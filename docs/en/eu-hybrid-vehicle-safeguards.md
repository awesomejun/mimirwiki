# EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards

> EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards are provisional European Union import quotas taking effect on December 1, 2026, for new hybrid (HEV, including mild hybrids) and plug-in hybrid (PHEV) passenger cars from all origins. China receives a 191,780-vehicle quota through June 18, 2027 (350,000 annualized), and imports exceeding allocated quotas are prohibited while the Commission completes its investigation.

EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards are provisional quantitative import restrictions introduced by the European Commission under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2614, published on November 24, 2026, and entering into force on December 1, 2026, to address a sharp increase in imports of certain new hybrid passenger cars. Applying regardless of country of origin, the safeguard covers full hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs, including mild hybrids) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), while excluding range-extended electric vehicles (EREVs) whose combustion engine only generates electricity. Under the provisional regime running from December 1, 2026, through June 18, 2027, China is allocated a 200-day quota of 191,780 vehicles following an October 9, 2026 EU-China consultation understanding targeting 350,000 vehicles annualized, and shipments exceeding a country's quota are not permitted to enter the EU. Final investigation findings and any longer-term definitive safeguard measures remain pending.

**Also known as:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2614, EU hybrid car import quotas

## EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards background and October 2026 investigation

The EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards stem from a safeguard investigation initiated by the European Commission on October 30, 2026, following an understanding reached during the second meeting of the EU-China Trade and Investment Consultations on October 9, 2026.[1][4] According to the Commission and Eurometal, EU imports of hybrid passenger cars rose significantly after 2023 while EU domestic manufacturers' market share declined, leading the Commission to conclude that rapid import growth posed an imminent threat of serious injury to the EU automotive industry.[4]

The Commission attributed the import surge in part to global excess production capacity and trade-restrictive measures in other major markets.[4] On November 24, 2026, the Commission published its provisional safeguard measures (IP/26/2564), scheduled to apply from December 1, 2026.[1][2][3]

## EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards covered vehicle types and country allocations

According to the European Commission's November 24, 2026 announcement, the provisional safeguards cover two vehicle categories: full hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), including mild hybrids, and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs).[1][5] CarNewsChina reported that range-extended electric vehicles (EREVs), whose combustion engine only generates electricity, fall outside the hybrid quota and remain subject to the EU's battery-electric vehicle countervailing tariff framework.[5]

Because WTO safeguard rules require non-discriminatory application across origins, the European Commission allocated quotas in two tiers: country-specific quotas for origins accounting for more than 2% of EU hybrid imports in 2025, and a residual quota for all other origins.[1][3] For most countries with country-specific quotas, allocations were set at 2025 import volumes increased by slightly over 15%, covering Japan, Turkey, the United Kingdom, South Korea, Mexico, Morocco, Serbia, and the United States in addition to China.[1][3] China's provisional quota for the December 1, 2026 to June 18, 2027 period is 191,780 vehicles, reflecting the 350,000-vehicle annualized understanding reached with Beijing.[3][4][5]

## EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards quota enforcement and international reactions

Unlike tariff-rate quotas that allow additional shipments at a higher duty rate, the EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards establish hard quantitative ceilings: once an origin's quota is exhausted, imports beyond the quota are not permitted.[4] In the United Kingdom, the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders (SMMT) stated it was assessing the implications and urged that final arrangements recognize the UK-EU automotive relationship without creating new barriers.[3]

On November 26, 2026, China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) spokesperson He Yadong stated that China would closely follow the EU's subsequent investigation and safeguard the legitimate rights and interests of Chinese industries, recalling the prior pre-initiation consultations between China and the EU.[6]

## EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards pending final investigation

The measures taking effect on December 1, 2026, are provisional safeguards lasting through June 18, 2027, while the European Commission conducts its full investigation.[4][5] As of November 27, 2026, the Commission's final investigative findings and whether longer-term definitive safeguard measures will be adopted remain unconfirmed.[1][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### Which hybrid vehicles are covered by the EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards?

The provisional safeguards cover new full hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs, including mild hybrids) and plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs). Range-extended electric vehicles (EREVs) whose combustion engine only generates electricity are excluded from the quota and treated under the EU's all-electric vehicle tariff rules. [1][5]

### Which countries of origin are subject to the EU hybrid vehicle import quotas?

The measure applies globally to all origins from December 1, 2026. Countries accounting for more than 2% of 2025 EU imports receive country-specific quotas (including China, Japan, Turkey, the UK, South Korea, Mexico, Morocco, Serbia, and the United States), while all other origins share a residual quota. [1][3]

### What happens if imports exceed the allocated EU hybrid vehicle quota?

Because the provisional measure operates as an absolute quantitative quota rather than a tariff-rate quota, imports beyond an origin's allocated quota are not permitted to enter the EU. [4]

### How large is China's provisional quota under the EU Hybrid Vehicle Safeguards?

China is allocated 191,780 vehicles for the provisional period from December 1, 2026, through June 18, 2027, corresponding to an annualized cap of 350,000 vehicles following October 2026 EU-China consultations. [3][4][5]
