# H3 Launch Vehicle

> The H3 Launch Vehicle is Japan's two-stage liquid-fueled flagship orbital rocket jointly developed by JAXA and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) to succeed H-IIA and H-IIB. On November 20, 2026, JAXA and MHI agreed to transfer legal launch operator responsibility for H3-22 rockets with LE-9 Type 1A engines to MHI starting with Flight 11, scheduled for January 11, 2027.

The H3 Launch Vehicle is Japan's two-stage, liquid-fueled flagship orbital rocket jointly developed since 2014 by the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) to replace the H-IIA and H-IIB launch vehicles. While MHI has provided commercial launch transport services since Flight 4, JAXA previously retained legal responsibility as the official launch operator under Japan's Space Activities Act. On November 20, 2026, JAXA and MHI announced an agreement to transfer launch operations for the H3-22 configuration equipped with LE-9 Type 1A first-stage main engines to MHI in stages, beginning with Flight 11 (H3-F11), which MHI scheduled for January 11, 2027, from Tanegashima Space Center carrying a Japanese government information-gathering satellite.

## H3 development history and flight record

Development of the H3 Launch Vehicle began in 2014 to replace Japan's H-IIA and H-IIB rockets, with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries serving as prime contractor coordinating vehicle development and co-developing the engine system with JAXA.[3][4] After LE-9 engine qualification issues delayed the maiden launch from 2021 to 2023, the first test vehicle (TF1) failed in March 2023 due to a second-stage engine ignition failure.[3]

Following corrective measures, Flights 2 through 5 succeeded.[3] According to November 2026 Jiji Press reporting in The Japan Times, the H3 program also experienced a failure with Flight 8 in December 2025, then achieved three consecutive successful launches prior to the November 2026 operational transfer decision.[6]

## Who handles rocket development vs. launch service operations

In its November 20, 2026 announcement, JAXA detailed how development and launch operator responsibilities are divided between JAXA and MHI.[1] Since Flight 4, MHI had already provided launch transport services, while JAXA served as the statutory launch operator under Japan's Act on Launching of Spacecraft, etc. and Control of Spacecraft.[1]

| Function | Prior Arrangement (Flights 4-10) | Phased Transfer (From Flight 11, H3-22 Type 1A) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch transport service | Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) | Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) |
| Legal launch operator | JAXA | Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (MHI) |
| LE-9 Type 2 & rocket upgrades | JAXA (with MHI) | JAXA continues development; staged handover planned |

Under the agreement, MHI assumes both launch transport service provision and statutory launch operator duties for the H3-22 configuration powered by LE-9 Type 1A engines.[1][7] JAXA will continue developing the LE-9 Type 2 engine and pursuing H3 performance and operability upgrades, while other configurations and Type 2 launches are planned to transition to private-sector operations in stages following stakeholder consultations.[1]

## H3 Flight 11 launch plan under MHI operations

On November 20, 2026, MHI announced that Flight 11 (H3-F11) will carry the Japanese government's Optical Time-Axis Diversification 1 information-gathering satellite, operated by the Cabinet Satellite Intelligence Center.[2][5] The launch is scheduled for January 11, 2027, with a launch window from 08:00 to 10:00 JST at the Tanegashima Space Center in Kagoshima Prefecture.[5]

## H3 unverified milestones as of November 2026

As of November 27, 2026, the actual execution of H3 Flight 11 under MHI's legal launch operator responsibility and the exact dates for expanding private-sector handover to additional H3 configurations and LE-9 Type 2 launches remain unconfirmed.[1][5]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the H3 Launch Vehicle?

The H3 Launch Vehicle is Japan's two-stage liquid-fueled flagship orbital rocket jointly developed by JAXA and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries since 2014 to succeed the H-IIA and H-IIB rockets. [3][6]

### Who handles H3 rocket development and who operates launch services?

JAXA and MHI jointly develop the rocket, with JAXA continuing LE-9 Type 2 engine development and vehicle upgrades. For launch operations, MHI has provided transport services since Flight 4 and, under the November 20, 2026 agreement, also takes over legal launch operator duties for H3-22 (LE-9 Type 1A) launches starting with Flight 11. [1][3]

### When is MHI's first H3 launch as legal launch operator scheduled?

MHI scheduled H3 Flight 11 (H3-F11) for January 11, 2027, between 08:00 and 10:00 JST from the Tanegashima Space Center, carrying a Japanese government information-gathering satellite. [2][5]

### Are all H3 configurations transferred to MHI at once?

No. The initial transfer covers only the H3-22 configuration using LE-9 Type 1A engines; other configurations and launches using the LE-9 Type 2 engine are planned to shift to private-sector operations in stages after further consultations. [1]
