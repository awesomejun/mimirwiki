# Ibuprofen

> Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to treat pain, inflammation, and fever, available over the counter in 200 mg doses and by prescription in 400 mg, 600 mg, and 800 mg tablets. In November 2026, GSMS voluntarily recalled one U.S. prescription lot (GS069303, NDC 51407-841-05) of 800 mg tablets over potential surface aluminum particles.

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) with analgesic and antipyretic properties used to relieve pain, inflammation, and fever. Chemically identified as (±)-2-(p-isobutylphenyl) propionic acid, ibuprofen is marketed over the counter in lower-strength 200 mg doses and by prescription in 400 mg, 600 mg, and 800 mg oral tablets. Prescription 800 mg tablets are indicated for signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis, mild to moderate pain, and primary dysmenorrhea. In November 2026, Golden State Medical Supply, Inc. (GSMS) issued a voluntary nationwide consumer-level recall for a single prescription lot (GS069303) of 800 mg ibuprofen tablets due to potential aluminum particulate contamination on the tablet surface.

## Ibuprofen chemistry, pharmacology, and clinical uses

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) whose active ingredient is (±)-2-(p-isobutylphenyl) propionic acid, a white powder with a melting point of 74 to 77 degrees Celsius that is very slightly soluble in water and readily soluble in organic solvents such as ethanol and acetone.[3] According to U.S. prescribing information hosted on DailyMed, ibuprofen exhibits analgesic and antipyretic activities, and its mode of action, like that of other NSAIDs, is not completely understood but may be related to prostaglandin synthetase inhibition.[3]

Ibuprofen is available over the counter in 200 mg doses and by prescription in 400 mg, 600 mg, and 800 mg oral tablets.[3][6] According to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) recall notice and DailyMed labeling, 800 mg prescription ibuprofen tablets are indicated for relief of the signs and symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis, mild to moderate pain, and treatment of primary dysmenorrhea.[1][3]

## Ibuprofen cardiovascular and gastrointestinal warnings

Ibuprofen carries FDA boxed warnings shared across non-aspirin NSAIDs regarding cardiovascular and gastrointestinal risks.[2][3] According to DailyMed prescribing information and MedlinePlus, NSAIDs increase the risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, including myocardial infarction (heart attack) and stroke, which can be fatal and may occur early in treatment or increase with longer duration or higher doses.[2][3] Ibuprofen tablets are contraindicated in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery.[2][3]

NSAIDs including ibuprofen also increase the risk of serious gastrointestinal adverse events, including bleeding, ulceration, and perforation of the esophagus, stomach, or intestines, which can occur at any time during treatment without warning symptoms and can be fatal.[2][3] DailyMed and MedlinePlus note that older adults face higher risk, and MedlinePlus lists concurrent use of anticoagulants, aspirin, other NSAIDs, oral steroids, and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), as well as smoking or heavy alcohol use, among factors that affect gastrointestinal risk.[2][3]

## Ibuprofen November 2026 GSMS 800 mg tablet recall

On November 23, 2026, Golden State Medical Supply, Inc. (GSMS), based in Camarillo, California, initiated a voluntary nationwide consumer-level recall of one lot of prescription Ibuprofen 800 mg Tablets due to potential aluminum particulate contamination localized on the surface of the tablets; the FDA published the company announcement on November 24, 2026.[1][4]

| Recall Field | Official Details |
| --- | --- |
| Brand and Product | GSMS Ibuprofen 800 mg Tablets |
| National Drug Code (NDC) | 51407-841-05 |
| Lot Number | GS069303 |
| Expiration Date | 01/2030 |
| Tablet Appearance | White, oval-shaped tablets imprinted with "8I" on one side |

The recall applies strictly to Lot GS069303 and does not affect over-the-counter 200 mg ibuprofen tablets.[1][6] The Hill reported that the affected lot was sold to medical supply distributor McKesson, which was advised to quarantine affected stock and return it to GSMS.[5] According to the FDA-posted notice, continued use of tablets from the recalled lot may cause minor irritation and abrasion of the gastrointestinal tract, such as stomach or intestinal linings, though the likelihood of an adverse event is considered remote given the particle size of 0.01 to 0.10 millimeters.[1][4] As of the November 23, 2026 announcement, GSMS stated it was not aware of any adverse events associated with Lot GS069303.[1]

## Ibuprofen recall instructions and unverified developments

In the November 24, 2026 FDA-posted company announcement, GSMS instructed consumers, patients, and caregivers holding tablets from Lot GS069303 to stop using the medication, contact a physician, pharmacist, or healthcare provider, and return any unused portion to the pharmacy where it was obtained.[1][4] The Hill reported that inquiries to GSMS regarding the recall are directed to `recalls@gsms.us`.[5]

As of November 26, 2026, final completion of the recall and whether any adverse events have been reported after the initial November 23 to 24 notices have not been published by the FDA or GSMS.[1]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is ibuprofen used for?

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to relieve pain, inflammation, and fever. Prescription 800 mg oral tablets are indicated for rheumatoid arthritis, osteoarthritis, mild to moderate pain, and primary dysmenorrhea. [1][3]

### Which ibuprofen product and lot number are included in the November 2026 U.S. recall?

The voluntary nationwide recall announced by Golden State Medical Supply, Inc. (GSMS) on November 23, 2026, and posted by the FDA on November 24, 2026, covers only GSMS prescription Ibuprofen 800 mg Tablets with NDC 51407-841-05, Lot Number GS069303, and expiration date 01/2030. [1]

### Are over-the-counter 200 mg ibuprofen tablets affected by the November 2026 recall?

No. The November 2026 GSMS recall applies strictly to one prescription-strength 800 mg lot (GS069303) and does not affect standard over-the-counter 200 mg ibuprofen products. [1][6]

### Why was GSMS Ibuprofen 800 mg Lot GS069303 recalled?

GSMS issued the voluntary recall due to potential contamination with microscopic aluminum particles (0.01 to 0.10 mm) on the surface of the tablets, which could cause minor irritation or abrasion of the stomach or intestinal linings, though the company and FDA described the likelihood of an adverse event as remote. [1][4]

### What major safety warnings apply to ibuprofen in general?

FDA prescribing information and MedlinePlus include boxed warnings that NSAIDs such as ibuprofen increase the risk of serious cardiovascular thrombotic events (heart attack and stroke) and serious gastrointestinal bleeding, ulceration, or perforation, and are contraindicated in the setting of coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) surgery. [2][3]
