# 5G Standalone (SA)

> Unlike 5G Non-Standalone (NSA), which pairs 5G radios with an LTE core, 5G Standalone (SA) runs both base stations and the core network on 5G, enabling network slicing and ultra-low-latency services. In South Korea, following KT (July 2021), LG Uplus launches nationwide 5G SA on 2026-11-30 and SK Telecom on 2026-12-01.

5G Standalone (5G SA) is a fifth-generation mobile network architecture built entirely on 5G technology from radio base stations through the core network, without relying on a 4G LTE core as in Non-Standalone (NSA) deployments. On 2026-11-26, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT and the nation's three mobile carriers announced nationwide 5G SA commercialization, with LG Uplus launching on 2026-11-30 and SK Telecom on 2026-12-01, joining KT which launched SA in July 2021.

## 5G Standalone architecture compared with Non-Standalone (NSA)

5G Standalone (5G SA) is a mobile communications architecture in which both the radio base stations transmitting signals and the core network processing them are built entirely on 5G technology.[2][3] By contrast, 5G Non-Standalone (NSA), which South Korean carriers adopted when launching 5G in April 2019, pairs 5G radio access with an existing 4G LTE core network.[4][5]

Because 5G SA operates independently of the LTE core, it supports core 5G capabilities such as ultra-low-latency, high-reliability communications and network slicing, which divides a single physical network into multiple virtual networks tailored to the speed, latency, and stability requirements of each service.[2][6]

## 5G Standalone nationwide commercialization in South Korea

5G Standalone nationwide deployment across all three South Korean mobile carriers was announced on November 26, 2026, at a joint public-private event held by the Ministry of Science and ICT with SK Telecom, KT, and LG Uplus at the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry in Seoul.[1][2] KT first commercialized 5G SA in South Korea in July 2021, while LG Uplus and SK Telecom operated on NSA until the government attached a 2026 SA transition requirement to spectrum reallocation.[4][5]

Under the schedule announced on November 26, 2026, LG Uplus begins nationwide 5G SA commercialization on November 30, 2026, and SK Telecom follows on December 1, 2026.[1][2] NewsTomato reported that KT has about 8.27 million SA-capable devices, while SK Telecom projected that 10 million to 14 million subscribers will be able to use 5G SA by late 2027.[4][7] Exact device and tariff-plan eligibility across all carriers remains subject to each operator's rollout details.[2]

## 5G Standalone network slicing and service applications

5G Standalone enables carriers and enterprises to deploy specialized communications services across public safety, industrial automation, and consumer scenarios.[2][6] At the November 26, 2026 event, the Ministry of Science and ICT and carriers highlighted applications including firefighting and emergency response, dedicated enterprise security networks, remote control of robots and drones, and stable live broadcasting in crowded concert venues.[2][6]

Digital Daily reported that carrier plans include dedicated virtual lanes for crowded-venue live streaming and dual-network separation on a single smartphone for corporate email versus general internet traffic.[8]

## 5G Standalone policy guidelines and quality evaluation

5G Standalone commercialization in South Korea is accompanied by updates to telecommunications regulation and quality measurement.[2][4] The Ministry of Science and ICT stated on November 26, 2026, that it will maintain core net neutrality principles protecting general internet quality and prohibiting unfair prioritization of specific apps or content, while clarifying recognition criteria for specialized services requiring guaranteed quality.[2]

In addition, the ministry plans to conduct pilot SA quality measurements in December 2026 and overhaul its network quality evaluation framework in 2027 to shift from download-speed-only metrics toward user-experienced indicators.[2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### How is 5G Standalone (SA) different from 5G Non-Standalone (NSA)?

5G Non-Standalone (NSA) combines 5G base stations with an existing 4G LTE core network, whereas 5G Standalone (SA) builds both the base stations and the core network entirely on 5G technology, enabling network slicing and ultra-low-latency services. [2][3]

### When do South Korea's three mobile carriers offer nationwide 5G Standalone (SA)?

KT launched 5G SA in July 2021, LG Uplus starts nationwide 5G SA commercialization on November 30, 2026, and SK Telecom launches on December 1, 2026. [1][2]

### What is network slicing in 5G Standalone?

Network slicing is a 5G SA capability that divides a single physical mobile network into multiple virtual networks to deliver customized speed, latency, and reliability for specific services such as emergency response, robots, or crowded-venue broadcasting. [2][6]

### How is the South Korean government updating regulations for 5G Standalone?

The Ministry of Science and ICT announced it will clarify criteria for specialized quality-guaranteed services within net neutrality guidelines and overhaul its telecom quality evaluation system starting with December 2026 pilot measurements. [2][4]
