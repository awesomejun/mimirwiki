# Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974

> Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 empowers the U.S. Trade Representative to investigate unreasonable foreign trade practices that restrict U.S. commerce and propose responsive tariffs. On November 25, 2026, USTR made affirmative determinations and proposed tariffs across 14 trading partners over manufacturing excess capacity, opening comments until January 5, 2027, and hearings starting January 25, 2027.

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 is a U.S. trade statute that authorizes the Office of the United States Trade Representative (USTR) to investigate foreign government acts, policies, and practices that are unreasonable or discriminatory and burden or restrict U.S. commerce, and to propose and impose responsive trade actions such as additional tariffs. Under Section 301(b), an affirmative determination of actionable practices is followed by a proposed action, a public comment period, and public hearings before any final duties take effect. On November 25, 2026, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer issued affirmative determinations and proposed broad-based tariffs in 14 Section 301 investigations concerning structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors, covering economies including China, the European Union, Japan, South Korea, and Vietnam. Written comments are due January 5, 2027, with public hearings scheduled to begin on January 25, 2027, before final tariff rates, exemptions, and effective dates are decided.

## Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 authority and procedural stages

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 authorizes the United States Trade Representative to investigate foreign acts, policies, and practices and determine under Section 301(b) whether they are unreasonable and burden or restrict U.S. commerce.[1][2] A Section 301 proceeding moves through distinct procedural stages rather than imposing tariffs immediately upon investigation.[2][7]

First, USTR initiates an investigation via a Federal Register notice.[4] Second, USTR publishes economy-specific investigative reports and issues an affirmative determination if practices are found actionable under Section 301(b).[1][3] Third, USTR publishes a Notice of Proposed Action detailing proposed tariffs and exemptions, opens a public written comment period, and convenes public hearings before the Section 301 Committee.[2][7] Only after reviewing public comments and testimony does USTR issue a final determination setting final tariff rates, product exclusions, and effective dates.[2][7]

## Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 November 2026 excess-capacity determinations

On November 25, 2026, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer announced affirmative Section 301(b) determinations and proposed responsive actions across 14 investigations relating to structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors.[1][5] The 14 covered trading partners are Bangladesh, Cambodia, China, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam.[1][5] USTR stated that it had not made determinations regarding Mexico or Norway as of November 25, 2026.[1]

USTR released economy-specific reports supporting each determination, citing industrial strategies such as manufacturing targeting, wage and labor suppression, financial support, non-market economy inputs, and other market-distorting practices that USTR found burden or restrict U.S. commerce.[1][3] Wiley reported that USTR is proposing broad-based tariffs on imports from the 14 economies subject to product-specific exemptions, with proposed rates varying by partner and in many cases reflecting commitments under existing bilateral trade agreements or frameworks.[7]

## Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 Korea notice and hearing schedule

In its November 25, 2026 Federal Register Notice for Korea (Docket Nos. USTR-2026-0741 and USTR-2026-0734), USTR determined that Korea's acts, policies, and practices relating to structural excess manufacturing capacity are actionable under Section 301 and proposed additional duties on products of Korea subject to specified exceptions.[2] The Korea Times reported that the proposed U.S. tariff framework maintains an aligned 15% rate for South Korea while issuing warnings regarding semiconductors and bilateral commitments.[6]

Under the Korea Federal Register Notice, USTR set January 5, 2027, as the deadline for both written public comments and requests to appear at the public hearing with a testimony summary.[2] The Section 301 Committee is scheduled to convene public hearings starting at 10:00 a.m. on January 25, 2027, in the main hearing room of the U.S. International Trade Commission in Washington, D.C.[2]

## Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 pending final decisions

As of November 27, 2026, the tariffs announced on November 25, 2026, remain proposed actions rather than duties in force.[2][7] Final tariff rates, product-specific exemptions, possible higher alternative rates, and effective enforcement dates for each of the 14 economies have not yet been determined and will follow the January 2027 comment and hearing process.[2][7]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974?

Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 is a U.S. law authorizing the U.S. Trade Representative (USTR) to investigate foreign government acts, policies, and practices that are unreasonable and burden or restrict U.S. commerce, and to take responsive trade actions such as additional tariffs. [1][2]

### How do the determination, tariff proposal, and actual tariff imposition stages differ under Section 301?

First, USTR investigates and makes an affirmative determination under Section 301(b) that foreign practices are actionable. Next, USTR publishes a proposed tariff action and gathers public comments and hearing testimony. Only after that review can USTR finalize rates, exemptions, and an effective date for actual tariff collection. [1][2][7]

### Which 14 economies were covered by USTR's November 25, 2026 Section 301 determinations?

USTR issued affirmative determinations for Bangladesh, Cambodia, China, the European Union, India, Indonesia, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand, and Vietnam. No determinations were made at that time for Mexico or Norway. [1]

### What is the comment and hearing schedule for the November 2026 Section 301 proposals?

Written comments and requests to testify are due January 5, 2027, and the Section 301 Committee will begin public hearings on January 25, 2027, at the U.S. International Trade Commission in Washington, D.C. [2]
