# OpenAI

> Founded in 2015 as a nonprofit and restructured on October 28, 2025, into the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation controlling the for-profit OpenAI Group PBC, OpenAI develops frontier AI models including GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, ChatGPT, and Codex. As of the recapitalization close, the OpenAI Foundation holds a 26% equity stake (~$130 billion valuation plus a 15-year performance warrant), Microsoft holds roughly 27%, and employees and investors hold 47%, with the Foundation appointing all Group directors and committing an initial $25 billion toward health breakthroughs and AI resilience.

OpenAI is a San Francisco-based artificial intelligence research and deployment organization founded in 2015 with the stated mission of ensuring that artificial general intelligence (AGI) benefits all of humanity. Following a structural recapitalization announced on October 28, 2025, OpenAI operates through the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation and its controlled for-profit subsidiary, OpenAI Group PBC (a public benefit corporation), developing the ChatGPT platform, Codex, and the GPT-6, GPT-5, GPT-Image, GPT-Live, and open-weight gpt-oss model families.

## History, AGI Mission, and the OpenAI Charter

OpenAI was established in 2015 as a nonprofit research organization dedicated to ensuring that artificial general intelligence (AGI), defined in the OpenAI Charter as highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work, benefits all of humanity.[2][4] Its GitHub organization (`github.com/openai`) was created on October 3, 2015, and hosts 280 public repositories as of late 2026.[8] In 2019, OpenAI formed a for-profit subsidiary controlled by the original nonprofit board to raise capital and scale compute-intensive model training and deployment.[2]

The OpenAI Charter codifies four foundational operating principles governing the organization's work:[4]

1. **Broadly Distributed Benefits**: OpenAI commits to using any influence it obtains over AGI deployment to benefit humanity broadly, avoid uses that harm humanity or unduly concentrate power, and treat humanity as its primary fiduciary duty while minimizing stakeholder conflicts of interest.[4] 2. **Long-Term Safety**: OpenAI commits to conducting and sharing safety alignment research and includes a late-stage "stop competing and start assisting" clause: if a value-aligned, safety-conscious project comes close to building AGI before OpenAI (typically defined as a better-than-even chance of success within the next two years), OpenAI pledges to cease competing and assist that project instead.[4] 3. **Technical Leadership**: Reasoning that policy and safety advocacy alone are insufficient without being on the cutting edge of AI capabilities, OpenAI strives to lead in pre-AGI capabilities directly aligned with its mission.[4] 4. **Cooperative Orientation**: OpenAI commits to collaborating with external research and policy institutions and providing public goods, while acknowledging that security and safety considerations will gradually shift its output from traditional open capabilities publishing toward sharing safety, policy, and standards research.[4]

## 2025 PBC Recapitalization, Board Governance, and Ownership

On October 28, 2025, following nearly a year of formal discussions with the Offices of the Attorneys General of California and Delaware, OpenAI completed a comprehensive recapitalization that simplified its corporate architecture while keeping the nonprofit in control of the commercial enterprise:[2][3]

- **The OpenAI Foundation (Controlling Nonprofit)**: The parent 501(c)(3) nonprofit was renamed the OpenAI Foundation.[2][3] Holding exclusive special voting and governance rights, the OpenAI Foundation appoints every member of the for-profit board of directors and retains unilateral authority to replace any director at any time.[2]
- **OpenAI Group PBC (Controlled Public Benefit Corporation)**: The for-profit subsidiary was converted into a Delaware public benefit corporation named OpenAI Group PBC, legally binding the commercial entity to advance the exact same mission as the OpenAI Foundation and to weigh the broader interests of all stakeholders alongside commercial growth.[2][3]

Under the recapitalization terms unanimously approved by the board, all previous complex profit-cap units were converted into conventional common equity in OpenAI Group PBC that participates proportionally in enterprise value growth:[2]

| Equity Holder Category | Ownership Share at Recapitalization Close | Valuation / Terms & Rights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OpenAI Foundation (Nonprofit) | 26% + Performance Warrant | Valued at ~$130 billion at close (implying ~$500B total valuation), plus a 15-year warrant granting significant additional equity if share price increases >10x |
| Microsoft Corporation | ~27% | Long-term strategic partner under multi-year cloud and IP agreements disclosed in Microsoft's FY2026 Form 10-K |
| Current/Former Employees & Investors | ~47% | Conventional proportional common stock |

With roughly $130 billion in initial equity value, the OpenAI Foundation launched an initial $25 billion philanthropic commitment focused on two pillars: accelerating health breakthroughs and curing diseases (including open-sourced frontier health datasets and scientific grants) and building technical solutions for AI resilience (protecting power grids, hospitals, financial systems, and critical infrastructure from AI-related risks), expanding upon its $50 million People-First AI Fund.[3]

The OpenAI Foundation is governed by an 11-member board of directors consisting of Chair Bret Taylor, CEO Sam Altman, Adam D'Angelo, Paul Christiano, Dr. Sue Desmond-Hellmann, Dr. Zico Kolter, Retired U.S. Army General Paul M. Nakasone, Adebayo Ogunlesi, Nicole Seligman, David Velez, and Robin Vince.[2] To prevent fragmented governance while preserving independent safety oversight, the Safety and Security Committee (SSC) remains a standing committee of the OpenAI Foundation overseeing all safety practices across both the Foundation and OpenAI Group PBC.[2] SSC Chair Dr. Zico Kolter serves exclusively as a voting director on the OpenAI Foundation board and sits on the OpenAI Group PBC board only as a non-voting observer (with a second Foundation director scheduled to transition to exclusive Foundation voting service and Group non-voting observer status within one year of the recapitalization).[2]

## Model Lineup and Product Ecosystem (GPT-6.2 Era)

By November 2026, OpenAI's API and product catalog is anchored by the GPT-6 and GPT-6.2 celestial-tier model series (`Astra`, `Sol`, `Terra`, and `Luna`), delivered primarily through the Responses API and official client SDKs alongside specialized multimodal, audio, life sciences, and open-weight releases:[5][6]

- **GPT-6.2 & GPT-6 Reasoning and Agentic Series**: Led by `gpt-6.2-astra` (released November 17, 2026, for complex start-to-finish projects and frontier coding) and `gpt-6.2-sol` (everyday coding and agent scaling), alongside `gpt-6.1-sol`, `gpt-6.1-terra` (fast visual reasoning, computer use, and natural writing), `gpt-6-astra`, `gpt-6-sol`, and `gpt-6-luna` (ultra-low-cost, high-volume tasks).[5][6] All current flagship text models accept text and image inputs, output text, and support configurable context caching with 30-minute and 12-hour cache write retention options.[5][6]
- **Domain-Specialized Frontier Models**: OpenAI maintains restricted-access domain models including `gpt-6-cyber` and `gpt-5.6-cyber` (for authorized vulnerability research, reproduction, and patch verification) and `gpt-rosalind-research` (GPT-Rosalind, providing life sciences and medicinal chemistry reasoning for approved organizations).[6]
- **Previous-Generation & Open-Weight Models**: The catalog retains `gpt-5.6-sol`, `gpt-5.6-terra`, `gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-5.5` and `gpt-5.5-pro`, `gpt-5.4` (`pro`, `mini`, `nano`), `gpt-5.2` (`pro`), `gpt-5.1`, `gpt-5`, `gpt-4.1`, `gpt-4o`, and `o3`/`o3-pro`, as well as two open-weight models: `gpt-oss-120b` (designed to fit onto a single NVIDIA H100 GPU) and `gpt-oss-20b` (a medium-sized low-latency open-weight model).[5][6]
- **Image, Voice, and Realtime Multimodal Models**: Visual generation is served by `gpt-image-2.6-sunburst`, `gpt-image-2.6-flare`, `gpt-image-2.5-sunburst`, and `gpt-image-2.5-flare`.[6] Audio and real-time speech workloads are served by `gpt-live-1.1` (full-duplex multilingual voice), `gpt-live-1`, `gpt-live-transcribe`, `gpt-realtime-2.1` and `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini` (reasoning voice models with tool use), `gpt-realtime-translate`, `gpt-realtime-whisper`, `gpt-audio-1.5`, and `gpt-transcribe`.[6]

On the application layer, OpenAI operates ChatGPT across consumer plans, ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise, and ChatGPT for Education, alongside the Codex software engineering agent platform, Dots, and vertical initiatives such as Astra for Law and media partnerships with publishers including Le Groupe Figaro and LVMH's Media Division.[2][3]

## API Pricing Architecture, Usage Tiers, and Web Crawlers

OpenAI structures its API pricing across four execution speed modes: Standard, Batch (50% discount for asynchronous 2-hour or 8-hour completion windows), Fast (2x standard price), and Ultrafast (6x standard price on eligible GPT-6 models).[5][7] Legacy Flex beta processing remains active only on older models (up to GPT-6.1 Sol/Terra and GPT-6 Astra) and is not offered on GPT-6.2.[5]

API rate limits (measured across RPM, RPD, TPM, TPD, IPM, and vector store limits of 300 RPM per store) scale across four named usage tiers based on cumulative credit purchases in supported countries and territories:[7]

- **Free Tier**: Allowed geographies only; $100 / month usage cap.[7]
- **Build Tier**: Unlocked at $5 in total credit purchases; $500 / month usage limit.[7]
- **Launch Tier**: Unlocked at $100 in total credit purchases; $5,000 / month usage limit.[7]
- **Grow Tier**: Unlocked at $500 in total credit purchases; $200,000 / month usage limit.[7]

For web data ingestion and real-time search, OpenAI operates four distinct HTTP user agents with independent `robots.txt` controls and published JSON IP allowlists: `GPTBot/1.4` (`openai.com/gptbot.json`, which crawls public web content for generative AI foundation model training), `OAI-SearchBot/1.4` (`openai.com/searchbot.json`, which indexes web pages for ChatGPT search results without training models), `OAI-AdsBot/1.0` (`openai.com/adsbot.json`, which validates policy compliance and relevance of landing pages submitted as advertisements on ChatGPT without training models), and `ChatGPT-User/1.0` (`openai.com/chatgpt-user.json`, triggered by live user queries and Custom GPT actions).[7]

In open-source ecosystem governance, OpenAI co-founded the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) on December 9, 2025, alongside Anthropic and Block, donating the `AGENTS.md` repository guidance specification as an inaugural project and joining Amazon Web Services, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Google, and Microsoft as an AAIF Platinum member.[9]

## Frequently asked questions

### How is OpenAI structured after its October 2025 recapitalization?

Since October 28, 2025, OpenAI consists of the nonprofit OpenAI Foundation and a controlled for-profit public benefit corporation called OpenAI Group PBC. The OpenAI Foundation holds exclusive special voting rights to appoint and replace all directors of OpenAI Group PBC, retains the Safety and Security Committee (chaired by Dr. Zico Kolter) at the Foundation level, and owns 26% of OpenAI Group's common equity (valued at roughly $130 billion at closing) plus a 15-year warrant for additional shares if the company's share price grows more than tenfold. [2]

### How much of OpenAI does Microsoft own?

According to OpenAI's structural disclosures following the October 2025 recapitalization, Microsoft holds approximately 27% of the equity in OpenAI Group PBC, the OpenAI Foundation holds 26% (plus a long-term valuation warrant), and current and former employees and other investors hold the remaining 47%. All stockholders hold conventional common stock that participates proportionally in OpenAI Group's value. [2]

### What are the main models in OpenAI's API lineup in late 2026?

OpenAI's flagship API models are GPT-6.2 Astra ($8/$40 per million short-context input/output tokens) for complex reasoning and coding, GPT-6.2 Sol ($2/$10) for everyday coding and scalable agents, GPT-6.1 Terra ($1/$5) for fast visual reasoning and computer use, and GPT-6 Luna ($0.10/$0.50) for high-volume workloads. OpenAI also offers domain-specific models (GPT-6 Cyber and GPT-Rosalind for life sciences), multimodal models (GPT-Image-2.6 Sunburst/Flare, GPT-Live-1.1, GPT-Realtime-2.1), and open-weight models (gpt-oss-120b and gpt-oss-20b). [6]

### What are the differences between OpenAI's web crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, OAI-AdsBot, and ChatGPT-User)?

OpenAI separates its web crawlers by function so site owners can set independent robots.txt rules: GPTBot/1.4 crawls content that may be used to train generative AI foundation models; OAI-SearchBot/1.4 indexes websites to surface them in ChatGPT search results; OAI-AdsBot/1.0 verifies policy compliance and relevance of landing pages submitted as ads on ChatGPT (without training models); and ChatGPT-User/1.0 fetches pages on demand when a user asks ChatGPT or a Custom GPT to visit a URL. [7]
