# AI in financial services

> AI in financial services covers financial firms' use of machine learning and generative AI across operations, IT, and decision-making, as well as resulting market-wide stability risks. In October 2026, 86% of surveyed UK financial firms reported using AI, and on November 26, 2026, the Bank of England warned that AI-related borrowing and shared dependencies could amplify systemic shocks.

AI in financial services refers to the use of machine learning, generative artificial intelligence, and automated decision systems by banks, insurers, asset managers, and payment providers across operations, IT, customer service, and risk management, together with the financial stability risks those deployments create. In a joint fourth survey published on October 26, 2026, the Bank of England and the UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) reported that 86% of respondent UK financial firms used AI, up from 75% in 2024, with generative AI accounting for 56% of reported systems. On November 26, 2026, the Bank of England's Financial Stability Report warned that deepening AI-related interconnections, heavy borrowing by AI infrastructure firms, and common third-party technology dependencies increase the likelihood that multiple financial risks could crystallize together. International standard-setters including the Financial Stability Board (FSB) published sound practices in October 2026 to guide responsible AI adoption across financial institutions.

## AI in financial services adoption and business functions

AI in financial services has expanded rapidly across UK financial institutions, according to the fourth joint survey by the Bank of England and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) published on October 26, 2026.[3] In that survey, 86% of respondent firms reported using AI, up from 75% in 2024.[3] The reported technology mix shifted sharply toward generative AI, which accounted for 56% of all reported AI systems in 2026 compared with 17% for foundation models in 2024.[3]

Reported deployments remained most concentrated in Operations and IT, representing approximately 31% of all reported AI systems, up from around 22% in 2024, while no other individual business area accounted for more than 11%.[3] Around 7% of reported AI systems were assessed by firms as high materiality, compared with a 16% share in 2024.[3]

## AI in financial services transmission from firm-level use to market-wide risk

In its November 26, 2026 Financial Stability Report and Financial Policy Committee (FPC) Record, the Bank of England outlined how firm-level AI adoption and AI sector financing can escalate into system-wide financial risk.[1][2] First, common and critical dependencies on a concentrated group of third-party technology, cloud, and model providers can turn an operational or cyber failure at one vendor into simultaneous disruption across multiple financial institutions.[1][6]

Second, deepening financial interconnections link AI infrastructure financing to broader credit and equity markets.[1][2] The FPC noted repeated swings in investor expectations regarding the future earnings of AI companies, contributing to higher bond yields and market volatility.[2] Crypto Briefing and StockAnalysis reported that the Bank of England's November 2026 report flagged more than $4 trillion in total debt and off-balance-sheet commitments tied to major AI hyperscalers, warning that stress in AI borrowing could spill over into sovereign bond and equity markets.[5][6]

## AI in financial services international governance and supervision

Regulators and international standard-setters have focused on operational resilience, third-party dependency oversight, and responsible governance for AI in financial services.[1][4] On October 15, 2026, the Financial Stability Board (FSB) finalized its sound practices for the responsible adoption of artificial intelligence across financial institutions.[4]

In the United Kingdom, the Bank of England's November 2026 Financial Stability Report stated that while the UK banking system remained resilient overall, cyber and operational risks had risen significantly and financial firms needed to strengthen resilience against AI-related operational and cyber threats.[1][6]

## AI in financial services unverified losses and future regulatory actions

As of November 27, 2026, the Bank of England's November 2026 assessment identifies forward-looking vulnerabilities rather than realized systemic losses.[1][2] Actual future financial losses from AI-related credit exposures or operational incidents, as well as specific binding supervisory rules to be adopted by national regulators in response to the November 2026 report, remain unconfirmed.[1][2]
