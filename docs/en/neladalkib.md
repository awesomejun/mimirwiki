# Neladalkib (Nelkarmi)

> On November 17, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granted accelerated approval to Nelkarmi (neladalkib, NDA 220936) at a recommended dosage of 150 mg orally once daily for adults with locally advanced or metastatic ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously received either one prior ALK TKI consisting of alectinib, or two or more prior ALK TKIs and platinum-based chemotherapy. Based on the Phase 1/2 ALKOVE-1 trial (NCT05384626), neladalkib achieved a 49% confirmed overall response rate (ORR; 55% intracranial ORR) in the post-alectinib cohort (N=67) and a 32% ORR (26% intracranial ORR) in the cohort with >=2 prior ALK TKIs plus platinum chemotherapy (N=155), with over half of responses lasting >=12 months.

Neladalkib (marketed under the brand name Nelkarmi and developed under the investigational code NVL-655) is an orally bioavailable, brain-penetrant, next-generation selective anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) originated by Nuvalent, Inc. and commercialized by GSK plc following GSK's $10.6 billion acquisition of Nuvalent in 2026. It is engineered to inhibit wild-type ALK fusions and single or compound ALK resistance mutations (including G1202R) across the blood-brain barrier while sparing tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) to minimize off-target central nervous system adverse events.

## Indication, Accelerated Approval, and Corporate Context

**Neladalkib** (brand name **Nelkarmi**, developed under the code **NVL-655**) is an oral anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) inhibitor granted **accelerated approval** by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on **November 17, 2026** (NDA 220936; CDER Novel Drug Therapy Approval #52 of 2026), ten days ahead of its original November 27, 2026 Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) target action date [1][2][3][7].

Nelkarmi is indicated for the treatment of adult patients with **locally advanced or metastatic ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)** who have previously received the following therapy in the unresectable locally advanced or metastatic setting [3][4]: - **One prior ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) consisting of alectinib**; or - **Two or more prior ALK TKIs and platinum-based chemotherapy**.

Neladalkib was originally designed by Cambridge, Massachusetts biotechnology firm **Nuvalent, Inc.**, which submitted NDA 220936 on March 27, 2026 with Breakthrough Therapy Designation, Orphan Drug Designation, Priority Review, and participation in the FDA Oncology Center of Excellence's Assessment Aid [3][5]. In mid-2026, following the presentation of Phase 1/2 ALKOVE-1 data at the ASCO Annual Meeting, British pharmaceutical company **GSK plc** (LSE/NYSE: `GSK`) acquired Nuvalent for **$10.6 billion** [8]. Nelkarmi became GSK's second FDA-approved lung cancer medicine from the Nuvalent acquisition in 2026, following the July 2026 approval of Jideytro for *ROS1*-positive NSCLC [7][8].

Because NDA 220936 was approved under the FDA's **Accelerated Approval** pathway (Section 506(c) of the FD&C Act and 21 CFR 314.510) based on overall response rate and duration of response, continued approval is contingent upon verification of clinical benefit in **Postmarketing Requirement PMR 5096-1**: completing the ongoing multicenter, randomized Phase 3 confirmatory trial (**Study NVL-655-04**) evaluating neladalkib in first-line ALK-positive locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC (scheduled for trial completion in December 2029 and final report submission in April 2030), alongside a deferred pediatric investigation (**PMR 5096-2**) for ages 2 to <17 years [5].

## Mechanism of Action: Brain Penetrance and TrkB Selectivity

*ALK* gene rearrangements occur in approximately **4% of NSCLC cases**, disproportionately affecting younger patients (often in their 40s and 50s) and non-smokers, with a high incidence of central nervous system (CNS) brain metastases [7][8]. While second-generation ALK TKIs (such as alectinib, brigatinib, and ensartinib) and the third-generation TKI lorlatinib achieve strong initial control, tumors frequently acquire single or compound secondary *ALK* kinase-domain resistance mutations (most notably the solvent-front mutation **G1202R**) [8][9]. Furthermore, lorlatinib inhibits off-target tropomyosin receptor kinase B (**TrkB**), which is associated with cognitive, mood, speech, and metabolic CNS adverse effects [8].

Neladalkib (`NVL-655`) was rationally macrocycle-engineered to address three simultaneous structural requirements [7][8][9]: 1. **Broad ALK Resistance Coverage**: Retains sub-nanomolar potency against wild-type *ALK* fusions as well as single and compound resistance mutations including **G1202R**, G1202R/L1196M, and G1202R/G1269A [7][9]. 2. **CNS Penetrance**: Crosses the blood-brain barrier to achieve high intracranial tumor concentrations [7]. 3. **TrkB-Sparing Selectivity**: Exhibits high selectivity for ALK over the TRK family (specifically sparing **TrkB**), minimizing off-target neurocognitive toxicity during long-term oral therapy [7][8].

**Dosage and Administration**: The recommended dosage of Nelkarmi is **150 mg taken orally once daily** with or without food at the same time every day until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [3][4]. Nelkarmi is supplied as **50 mg** (orange, debossed with `50` and `NLD`) and **150 mg** (red, debossed with `150` and `NLD`) film-coated oblong tablets that must be swallowed whole [4]. Concomitant use with strong or moderate **CYP3A inhibitors** or **CYP3A inducers** should be avoided [4].

## Pivotal Phase 1/2 ALKOVE-1 Trial (NCT05384626)

The accelerated approval of Nelkarmi was supported by the multicenter, single-arm, open-label, multi-cohort Phase 1/2 **ALKOVE-1** trial (`NCT05384626`) [3][6]. After Phase 1 dose escalation (15 mg to 200 mg daily) established the recommended Phase 2 dose of **150 mg once daily**, the FDA evaluated confirmed **overall response rate (ORR)** and **duration of response (DOR)** per RECIST v1.1 by blinded independent central review (BICR) across two primary labeled cohorts [3][7][9]:

| ALKOVE-1 Labeled Efficacy Cohort | Confirmed Systemic ORR (95% CI) | DOR >= 6 Months / >= 12 Months | Measurable CNS Intracranial ORR (CR Rate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 Prior ALK TKI Consisting of Alectinib** ($N = 67$) | **49%** (33/67; 95% CI: 37%-62%) | **76%** >= 6 mo / **52%** >= 12 mo | **55%** (11/20; 95% CI: 32%-77%; **25% CR**) [3][7] |
| **>= 2 Prior ALK TKIs + Platinum Chemotherapy** ($N = 155$) | **32%** (50/155; 95% CI: 24%-40%) | **69%** >= 6 mo / **51%** >= 12 mo | **26%** (14/53; 95% CI: 15%-40%; **11% CR**) [3][7] |

In the broader ASCO 2026 efficacy-evaluable ALK TKI-pretreated population ($N = 253$; median 3 prior lines of therapy, 40% baseline CNS metastases, 36% secondary *ALK* mutation, 19% *ALK* G1202R), neladalkib demonstrated a **46% ORR** (with an 80% 12-month DOR rate and **14.5-month median progression-free survival**) in lorlatinib-naive patients ($n = 63$) and a **26% ORR** (17.6-month median DOR) in heavily pretreated lorlatinib-experienced patients ($n = 190$) [8][9].

## Warnings, Mandatory Lab Monitoring, and Adverse Reactions

Before initiating Nelkarmi, clinicians must evaluate baseline **liver function tests (ALT, AST, and total bilirubin)**, **serum creatinine**, and **creatine phosphokinase (CPK)** [4]. The FDA Prescribing Information lists no contraindications (`None`) and specifies five Warnings and Precautions in Section 5 [3][4]: 1. **Hepatotoxicity (Section 5.1)**: Nelkarmi can cause severe and life-threatening hepatotoxicity, including drug-induced liver injury (DILI, Grade 3-4 in 0.3%) and hepatic failure (Grade 3-4 in 0.4%). In the pooled safety population, hepatotoxicity adverse events occurred in **56%** of patients (Grade 3 in 18%, Grade 4 in 7%), with laboratory ALT elevation in 62% and AST elevation in 63% (median time to onset: 16 days) [4]. Liver tests must be monitored at baseline, **every 2 weeks during the first 2 months**, and monthly thereafter [4]. 2. **Renal Toxicity (Section 5.2)**: Can cause severe renal toxicity, including acute kidney injury (1.1%; Grade 3-4 in 0.6%) and increased serum creatinine (24% on lab data; median onset 29 days). Monitor serum creatinine at baseline, **every 2 weeks for the first month**, and periodically thereafter [4]. 3. **Ocular Toxicity (Section 5.3)**: Occurred in **14%** of patients (median onset 43 days), including Grade 3-4 retinal detachment, corneal erosion, diplopia, and keratopathy. Patients with new or worsening visual symptoms must be promptly referred to an ophthalmologist or optometrist [4]. 4. **Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) Elevation (Section 5.4)**: Monitor serum CPK at baseline, **every 2 weeks for the first 2 months**, and periodically as clinically indicated [4]. 5. **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity (Section 5.5)**: Can cause fetal harm; females of reproductive potential must use effective **nonhormonal contraception** during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose, and patients are advised not to breastfeed [4].

Across clinical trials, the most common adverse reactions ($\ge 20\%$) were **hepatotoxicity, musculoskeletal pain, constipation, dysgeusia, peripheral neuropathy, edema, and fatigue**, while permanent discontinuation due to adverse reactions occurred in only **5%** of patients (most commonly for hepatotoxicity) [3][7].

## Frequently asked questions

### What is neladalkib (Nelkarmi) approved for?

On November 17, 2026, the U.S. FDA granted accelerated approval to Nelkarmi (neladalkib) for adults with locally advanced or metastatic ALK-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who have previously received either one prior ALK TKI consisting of alectinib, or two or more prior ALK TKIs and platinum-based chemotherapy. [1][3][4]

### What were the response rates of neladalkib in the ALKOVE-1 clinical trial?

In the Phase 1/2 ALKOVE-1 trial (NCT05384626), neladalkib 150 mg once daily achieved a 49% confirmed overall response rate (ORR) in patients whose only prior ALK TKI was alectinib (N=67, with 52% of responders maintaining a response for at least 12 months and a 55% intracranial ORR). In patients treated with two or more prior ALK TKIs and platinum chemotherapy (N=155), the confirmed ORR was 32% (51% lasting at least 12 months and a 26% intracranial ORR). [3][7][9]

### Why was neladalkib designed to spare the TrkB kinase?

Older brain-penetrant ALK inhibitors such as lorlatinib also inhibit tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) in the central nervous system, contributing to neurocognitive, mood, and speech side effects. Neladalkib (NVL-655) was engineered for high ALK selectivity and blood-brain barrier penetration while sparing TrkB to improve long-term CNS tolerability. [7][8]

### What laboratory tests must be monitored before and during Nelkarmi treatment?

Before starting Nelkarmi, clinicians must check liver function tests (ALT, AST, total bilirubin), serum creatinine, and creatine phosphokinase (CPK). Liver tests and CPK must be monitored every 2 weeks during the first 2 months (then monthly/periodically), and serum creatinine must be checked every 2 weeks during the first month, along with regular monitoring for visual changes. [4]

### Which companies developed and commercialize Nelkarmi?

Nelkarmi (neladalkib / NVL-655) was discovered and developed by Nuvalent, Inc., which was acquired by GSK plc in a $10.6 billion transaction in 2026. Nelkarmi is GSK's second FDA-approved lung cancer drug from the Nuvalent acquisition, following Jideytro (approved in July 2026 for ROS1-positive NSCLC). [1][7][8]
