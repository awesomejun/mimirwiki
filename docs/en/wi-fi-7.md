# Wi-Fi 7 (IEEE 802.11be)

> Wi-Fi 7 (IEEE 802.11be) is a wireless local area network generation featuring 320 MHz channel bandwidth, Multi-Link Operation across multiple frequency bands, and enhanced security. On November 26, 2026, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT announced the completion of Wi-Fi 7 upgrades on 32,847 city and metropolitan buses, raising monthly per-bus data to 330 GB and minimum guaranteed speed to 100 Mbps.

Wi-Fi 7 (based on the IEEE 802.11be wireless LAN standard) is a Wi-Fi generation that doubles maximum channel bandwidth from 160 MHz to 320 MHz, enables Multi-Link Operation (MLO) across multiple frequency bands simultaneously, and strengthens wireless security. On November 26, 2026, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT), together with the National Information Society Agency (NIA) and LG Uplus, announced the completion of Wi-Fi 7 access point upgrades across 32,847 city and metropolitan buses nationwide, increasing monthly data allowances per bus from 200 GB to 330 GB and raising minimum guaranteed speed (QoS) fivefold from 20 Mbps to 100 Mbps.

## Wi-Fi 7 architecture and smartphone support conditions

Wi-Fi 7 builds on IEEE 802.11be MAC and PHY enhancements overseen in the industry by the Wi-Fi Alliance.[3] Compared with Wi-Fi 6 and Wi-Fi 6E, Wi-Fi 7 doubles maximum channel width from 160 MHz to 320 MHz, supports simultaneous use of multiple frequency bands via Multi-Link Operation (MLO), and adds stronger security features to maintain stable connections in mobile environments.[4][7]

On upgraded public transit access points, passengers with older Wi-Fi smartphones can still connect and benefit from expanded backhaul data quotas and higher guaranteed speeds, while taking advantage of native Wi-Fi 7 capabilities such as 320 MHz channels and MLO requires a Wi-Fi 7-compatible handset.[7]

## Why South Korean bus Wi-Fi gets faster under the November 2026 upgrade

On November 26, 2026, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT), the National Information Society Agency (NIA), and LG Uplus announced that public Wi-Fi access points across 32,847 city and metropolitan buses nationwide had been replaced from Wi-Fi 6/6E to Wi-Fi 7.[1][2][7]

Speed and reliability improve not only because of Wi-Fi 7 access points, but also because the government raised both the monthly data cap (reducing throttling during busy commutes) and the minimum guaranteed speed fivefold.[2][7] The upgrade responded to rapidly rising passenger consumption: monthly average usage per bus climbed from 73 GB in 2022 to 102 GB in 2023, 165 GB in 2024, 197 GB in 2025, and 230 GB in September 2026, exceeding the old 200 GB cap.[6][7] In 2025, public bus Wi-Fi recorded about 10.56 billion cumulative connections and 74,994 TB of data traffic.[7]

## Public demonstration and quality check

To mark completion of the nationwide bus upgrade, MSIT held a two-day public experience event on November 25-26, 2026, at Sangam Culture Square in Seoul featuring a test bus equipped with the same Wi-Fi 7 environment, an exhibition zone explaining Wi-Fi 7 equipment, and live video playback and speed tests attended by MSIT, NIA, and LG Uplus officials.[2][5]
