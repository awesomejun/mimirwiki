# Asundexian

> Asundexian (marketed as Sunharbo) is an oral small-molecule activated factor XI (factor XIa) inhibitor developed by Bayer to prevent thrombotic stroke. On November 9, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Sunharbo 50 mg once-daily tablets in combination with antiplatelet therapy to reduce stroke risk in adults after a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke or high-risk transient ischemic attack. In the 12,327-patient Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, asundexian reduced ischemic stroke risk by 26% versus placebo without increasing overall major bleeding.

Asundexian (brand name Sunharbo; developmental code BAY 2433334) is an oral small-molecule anticoagulant developed by Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. that selectively inhibits activated coagulation factor XI (factor XIa, or FXIa). On November 9, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved Sunharbo 50 mg film-coated tablets under New Drug Application NDA221291 as the first FDA-approved factor XIa inhibitor, following earlier approval by China's National Medical Products Administration on October 26, 2026. In the United States, asundexian is indicated in combination with background antiplatelet therapy to reduce the risk of stroke in adults with a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score of 15 or lower) or a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA), based on the 12,327-participant Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial.

## Asundexian drug identity and approved medical indication

Asundexian (marketed in the United States as Sunharbo and studied under the developmental code BAY 2433334) is an orally administered small-molecule inhibitor of activated blood coagulation factor XI (factor XIa, or FXIa) developed by Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc.[1][3][4] In the U.S. Food and Drug Administration's openFDA and DailyMed registries, asundexian is cataloged under New Drug Application NDA221291, Unique Ingredient Identifier (UNII) `LA585UM8DE`, RxNorm Concept Unique Identifiers (RxCUI) `2756208` and `2756214`, Structured Product Labeling (SPL) Set ID `f48aee87-69a8-45a6-afa5-0917807efb70`, and National Drug Code (NDC) product code `50419-399` (package NDCs `50419-399-01`, `50419-399-05`, `50419-399-10`, and `50419-399-71`).[3][4]

On November 9, 2026, the FDA approved Sunharbo 50 mg film-coated tablets as a Type 1 New Molecular Entity and the first factor XIa inhibitor cleared in the United States.[1][2][3] Asundexian is indicated in combination with antiplatelet therapy to reduce the risk of stroke in adults with a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke (defined as a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score of 15 or lower caused by a clot that did not originate in the heart) or a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA).[1][4] The FDA explicitly noted that Sunharbo is not approved for stroke prevention in patients with atrial fibrillation.[1]

## Asundexian mechanism of action, pharmacology, and administration

Asundexian targets activated factor XI (FXIa), a serine protease in the intrinsic coagulation cascade.[4][9] In human blood coagulation, FXIa promotes thrombin generation via the intrinsic pathway, amplifying clot growth; by selectively binding and inhibiting FXIa, asundexian attenuates pathological thrombus amplification while preserving tissue-factor-driven hemostasis to a greater degree than direct factor Xa inhibitors such as rivaroxaban or apixaban.[4][9] Clinical development of FXIa inhibitors was motivated in part by human genetic observations that individuals with congenital factor XI deficiency exhibit lower rates of ischemic stroke and rarely experience spontaneous bleeding.[9]

According to Section 12 of the FDA-approved prescribing information for Sunharbo, oral administration of asundexian 50 mg once daily produces 97% inhibition of FXIa activity at peak plasma concentrations and 93% inhibition at trough concentrations.[4] Routine laboratory monitoring of coagulation parameters is not recommended during treatment.[4]

The recommended dosage of asundexian is 50 mg taken orally once daily with or without food.[4] For patients with dysphagia or enteral access, the tablet may be crushed and mixed with water or soft food for immediate oral intake, or suspended in 10 mL of non-carbonated water inside a syringe (either by shaking an intact tablet for 15 seconds every 30 minutes over 2 hours or by crushing and suspending the tablet) and flushed twice with 10 mL of water through a nasogastric or gastric feeding tube.[4] A prepared syringe suspension may be stored at 2 degrees C to 30 degrees C (36 degrees F to 86 degrees F) for up to 24 hours before administration.[4]

## Asundexian clinical efficacy and safety in the OCEANIC-STROKE trial

Regulatory approval of asundexian was based on OCEANIC-STROKE (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05686070), an international, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial sponsored by Bayer that enrolled 12,327 adults across 702 sites in 37 countries.[1][5][9] The study began on January 26, 2023, and reached primary completion on October 23, 2025.[5] Investigators presented late-breaking preliminary results (Abstract LB 009, led by principal investigator Mike Sharma of McMaster University and the Population Health Research Institute) at the American Stroke Association's International Stroke Conference in New Orleans on February 5, 2026, and published the full manuscript in *The New England Journal of Medicine* on April 15, 2026.[8][9]

OCEANIC-STROKE enrolled adults within 72 hours of symptom onset of a non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke (NIHSS score of 15 or lower) or high-risk TIA and randomized them 1:1 to receive asundexian 50 mg once daily (n = 6,162 efficacy; n = 6,124 safety) or matching placebo (n = 6,165 efficacy; n = 6,130 safety) on top of background antiplatelet therapy.[1][4] Across the trial population, 67% of participants received dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT, median duration 32 days), 87% received single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT, median duration 498 days), and roughly 30% received prophylactic-dose heparins or low-molecular-weight heparins (median duration 4 days).[4]

Over a median treatment duration of 14.2 months (median follow-up 19 months), asundexian reduced the primary endpoint of ischemic stroke by 26% relative to placebo (6.2% vs. 8.4%, hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.84).[1][4][8] Secondary coverage in STAT News and TCTMD noted an absolute risk reduction of 2.2 percentage points and a number needed to treat (NNT) of 53 patients for one year to prevent one recurrent stroke.[10][11]

## Asundexian bleeding subgroups, adverse reactions, and clinical precautions

The FDA-approved label for Sunharbo lists no contraindications, but documents subgroup bleeding variations, non-bleeding adverse reactions, drug interactions, and peri-procedural precautions.[4] While overall International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) major bleeding was comparable between asundexian and placebo (1.6 vs. 1.5 per 100 person-years; HR 1.1), the label and FDA announcement highlight elevated bleeding rates in specific settings:[1][4]

- First 90 days of therapy: ISTH major bleeding occurred at 4.5 versus 3.1 events per 100 person-years (HR 1.46, 95% CI 0.99 to 2.16).[4]
- Patients older than 75 years (25% of trial enrollment): ISTH major bleeding occurred at 2.6 versus 1.7 events per 100 person-years.[1][4]
- High-risk TIA subgroup (5% of trial enrollment): ISTH major bleeding occurred at 2.9 versus 1.0 events per 100 person-years, with a median onset of 30 days.[4]
- Planned DAPT versus non-DAPT: ISTH major bleeding was 1.9 versus 1.5 per 100 person-years in patients planned for DAPT, compared with 1.3 versus 1.4 per 100 person-years in those not planned for DAPT.[4]
- Gastrointestinal bleeding: occurred at 2.7 versus 2.1 events per 100 patient-years (HR 1.3, 95% CI 1.0 to 1.6); 74% of GI bleeds in the Sunharbo arm were minor or clinically relevant non-major (CRNM), most frequently of rectal origin.[1][4]

The most common non-bleeding adverse reaction (occurring in at least 3% of treated patients) was diarrhea, reported in 3.4% of the Sunharbo group versus 2.8% of the placebo group.[1][4] Hepatic transaminase elevations were also observed, with 74% classified as mild.[4]

Concomitant use of asundexian with combined P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and strong CYP3A4 inducers (such as carbamazepine) should be avoided because reduced plasma concentrations may decrease efficacy.[1][4] Asundexian is not recommended in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C), and patients are advised not to breastfeed during treatment and for 5 days after the final dose.[1][4] For invasive procedures, Sunharbo should be interrupted at least 24 hours prior to elective surgery and restarted after adequate hemostasis is established; in patients receiving thrombolysis (with or without thrombectomy) for acute ischemic stroke, Sunharbo should be withheld for at least 24 hours after thrombolysis.[1][4]

## Asundexian regulatory approvals, commercial availability, and pricing

Asundexian received its first regulatory approval on October 26, 2026, from China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), according to Bayer's regulatory disclosures.[6] Two weeks later, on November 9, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved NDA221291 under Fast Track and Priority Review designations (with the formal FDA approval letter dated November 10, 2026 in openFDA records and recorded as entry No. 48 on the FDA's 2026 Novel Drug Approvals list).[1][2][3] As of November 2026, marketing authorization applications for asundexian remained under regulatory review at the European Medicines Agency (EMA) and Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW).[6]

On November 20, 2026, Bayer announced that Sunharbo 50 mg tablets were commercially available to order by hospitals, health systems, and pharmacies in the United States in 30-count bottles and 100-count hospital packs.[4][7] As of 2026-11-20, Bayer set the U.S. wholesale acquisition cost (list price) of Sunharbo at $748.50 for a 30-day supply, alongside commercial co-pay support programs and a patient assistance foundation for eligible uninsured or underinsured patients.[7][11]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is asundexian (Sunharbo) and what is its FDA-approved indication?

Asundexian (marketed in the United States under the brand name Sunharbo) is an oral small-molecule inhibitor of activated coagulation factor XI (factor XIa, or FXIa) developed by Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc. On November 9, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Sunharbo 50 mg tablets in combination with antiplatelet therapy to reduce the risk of stroke in adults following a recent mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke (NIH Stroke Scale score of 15 or lower) or a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA). [1][3][4]

### How does asundexian work and what are its pharmacokinetic properties?

Asundexian selectively inhibits activated factor XI (FXIa), a serine protease in the intrinsic coagulation pathway that amplifies thrombin generation. According to the FDA-approved prescribing information, a 50 mg once-daily oral dose inhibits FXIa activity by 97% at peak concentrations and 93% at trough concentrations, possesses 94% absolute oral bioavailability and an elimination half-life of roughly 24 hours, and is metabolized primarily by carboxylesterase 1 (CES1) and to a lesser extent by CYP3A4. [4]

### What did the OCEANIC-STROKE clinical trial show for asundexian's efficacy and bleeding risk?

In the Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial (NCT05686070), which randomized 12,327 adults within 72 hours of a non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke or high-risk TIA across 702 sites in 37 countries, ischemic stroke occurred in 384 of 6,162 participants (6.2%) in the asundexian group versus 518 of 6,165 (8.4%) in the placebo group (hazard ratio 0.74, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.84; 26% relative risk reduction over a median 14.2 months of treatment). Overall ISTH major bleeding rates were 1.6 versus 1.5 events per 100 person-years (hazard ratio 1.1, 95% CI 0.8 to 1.4), though higher major bleeding rates were observed in patients over 75 years of age (2.6 vs. 1.7 per 100 person-years) and during the first 90 days of therapy (4.5 vs. 3.1 per 100 person-years). [1][4][5][8][9]

### How is asundexian dosed and administered?

The recommended dosage in the FDA label is one 50 mg film-coated tablet taken orally once daily with or without food, in combination with antiplatelet therapy. For patients unable to swallow intact tablets, the tablet may be crushed and mixed immediately with water or soft food, or suspended in 10 mL of non-carbonated water for administration via a nasogastric or gastric feeding tube. Sunharbo should be withheld for at least 24 hours prior to elective surgery or invasive procedures and for at least 24 hours following thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke. [1][4]

### What are the main precautions, drug interactions, and side effects of asundexian?

The FDA label lists no contraindications for Sunharbo, but advises avoiding concomitant use with combined P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and strong CYP3A4 inducers (such as carbamazepine), which can decrease asundexian exposure and effectiveness. Use is not recommended in patients with moderate or severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) or during breastfeeding (and for 5 days after the final dose), and the drug is not approved for stroke prevention in patients with atrial fibrillation. The most common non-bleeding adverse reaction in OCEANIC-STROKE was diarrhea (3.4% vs. 2.8% with placebo). [1][4]

### Where is asundexian approved and what is its US list price?

China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) approved asundexian on October 26, 2026, followed by the U.S. FDA under Fast Track and Priority Review designations on November 9, 2026 (NDA221291). As of November 2026, regulatory applications remained under review at the European Medicines Agency (EMA) and Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW). Bayer announced on November 20, 2026, that Sunharbo was available to order in the United States at a wholesale list price of $748.50 for a 30-day supply. [1][3][6][7][11]
