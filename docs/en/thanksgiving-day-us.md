## Thanksgiving Day federal holiday origins and statutory date

Thanksgiving Day is a U.S. federal holiday listed by the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) under federal holiday schedules, falling on Thursday, 2026-11-26 in 2026.[1]

According to the U.S. National Archives, the first Federal Congress passed a resolution on 1789-09-28 requesting that the President recommend a day of thanksgiving, and President George Washington proclaimed Thursday, 1789-11-26 as a "Day of Publick Thanksgivin."[2] Dates varied until President Abraham Lincoln's 1863 proclamation regularized annual observance on the last Thursday of November.[2] In 1939, when the last Thursday fell on the final day of November, President Franklin D. Roosevelt moved the observance to the second-to-last Thursday of November, prompting 32 states to follow while 16 states kept the last Thursday.[2] To resolve the two-date split, Congress passed a joint resolution (passed by the House on 1941-10-06 and amended by the Senate to the fourth Thursday of November) that President Roosevelt signed on 1941-12-26.[2]

## Thanksgiving Day 2026 and the 100th Macy's Parade

Thanksgiving Day on 2026-11-26 features the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City, created and produced by Macy's.[3] Macy's notes that the parade debuted in 1924 and has been held annually except for a three-year break during World War II, making the 2026 edition its 100th march.[3]

In its 2026-11-13 announcement, Macy's stated that the 100th parade is scheduled to air on Thursday, 2026-11-26 from 8:30 a.m. to 12:00 p.m. in all U.S. time zones on NBC and Telemundo and stream on Peacock.[3] Planned elements announced by Macy's include a record 100 inflatables, floats, Santa Claus, and Herald Square performances including Megan Moroney and Teddy Swims.[3] Actual balloon flight operations on parade morning depend on live conditions and are not confirmed as of 2026-11-25.[3]

## Thanksgiving Day 2026 travel forecast and weather outlook

Thanksgiving Day generates one of the largest annual domestic travel periods in the United States. On 2026-11-16, AAA projected that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home between Tuesday, 2026-11-24 and Monday, 2026-11-30.[4] That forecast represents a 0.1% decrease (about 121,000 fewer travelers) compared with 81.6 million in 2025, ending a three-year streak of record Thanksgiving travel that began in 2023.[4]

| Travel metric (AAA forecast, 2026-11-16) | 2026 projection | 2025 comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total domestic travelers (50+ miles) | 81.5 million | 81.6 million (-0.1%) |
| Travelers by car | 72.9 million (nearly 90%) | 73.1 million (-200,000) |

AAA Travel Vice President Stacey Barber stated that higher travel costs contributed to the slight decline, while projected volumes remain near record levels.[4] AAA noted that monthly national average gas prices set records in August, September, and October 2026, while travelers taking cruises, buses, and trains increased.[4] On 2026-11-23, TIME reported that stormy weather and coastal flooding were forecast in parts of the country during Thanksgiving travel week.[6] Final observed 2026 traveler totals are not yet available as of 2026-11-25.[4]
