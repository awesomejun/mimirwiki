# Digital Euro

> The digital euro is a retail central bank digital currency (CBDC) under preparation by the European Central Bank (ECB) and Eurosystem national central banks to complement physical cash across the euro area. In July 2026, the ECB selected 36 payment service providers for a 12-month technical pilot starting in the second half of 2027, while any final issuance depends on EU legislation.

The digital euro is a proposed electronic form of central bank cash being prepared by the European Central Bank (ECB) and Eurosystem national central banks for retail payments across the euro area. Designed to complement physical euro banknotes and coins rather than replace them, it would allow individuals and businesses to pay in shops, online, or person-to-person. On July 14, 2026, the ECB selected 36 payment service providers to participate in a 12-month technical and operational pilot scheduled to begin in the second half of 2027. Separately, the European Parliament voted on July 9, 2026, to open final trilogue negotiations on the digital euro legislative framework; as of November 27, 2026, final EU legislative adoption, any Governing Council decision to issue the digital euro, and a public launch date remain unconfirmed.

## Digital euro concept and intended use

The European Central Bank (ECB) defines the digital euro as a digital form of central bank cash intended to be available to everyone in the euro area for electronic payments in physical shops, online, and person-to-person.[3][4] The ECB states that a digital euro would complement euro banknotes and coins rather than replace physical cash.[3]

Alongside technical preparation, the ECB published version 0.91 of the draft digital euro scheme rulebook in July 2026 and opened a call for expression of interest for merchants to participate in testing.[3] The ECB's pilot FAQ also warns the public against fraudulent investment or participation offers claiming to sell or distribute digital euros.[2]

## Digital euro 36-provider pilot scheduled for H2 2027

On July 14, 2026, the ECB announced the selection of 36 payment service providers (PSPs) across the euro area to participate in the digital euro pilot, following a March 2026 call for expression of interest that drew more than 50 applications (with the annex list updated on September 15, 2026).[1] Reporting by Silicon Republic and EU Insider identified selected participants including Revolut, Stripe, UniCredit, Deutsche Bank, and SumUp.[5][6]

According to the ECB's pilot FAQ (updated October 28, 2026), the pilot is a limited practical exercise designed to evaluate technical, operational, and user readiness in real-life conditions and to gather evidence to inform future decisions.[2]

## How the digital euro pilot connects to EU legislation and issuance

The ECB's payment pilot tests technical and operational readiness, but it does not constitute a decision to issue the digital euro to the general public.[1][2] As documented in November 2026 legislative tracking, the European Parliament voted 416 to 169 (with 22 abstentions) on July 9, 2026, to open final trilogue negotiations on the digital euro legal framework.[7]

Under this two-track process, EU co-legislators negotiate the legal basis in trilogue while the Eurosystem prepares infrastructure and runs the 2027 pilot.[2][7] Industry analysis notes that a potential 2029 launch timeline remains contingent on completion of EU legislation and a subsequent formal issuance decision.[7]

## Digital euro unverified milestones as of November 2026

As of November 27, 2026, several core outcomes remain unconfirmed in the public primary record: final adoption of the EU digital euro regulation, whether the ECB Governing Council will formally decide to issue a digital euro, and any confirmed public launch date.[2][7] The ECB explicitly characterizes the H2 2027 exercise as a limited pilot to gather evidence for future decisions rather than a commercial rollout.[2]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the digital euro?

The digital euro is a proposed retail central bank digital currency (CBDC) being prepared by the European Central Bank and Eurosystem national central banks as an electronic form of central bank cash complementing banknotes and coins across the euro area. [3][4]

### How does the 2027 payment pilot connect to an actual issuance decision?

The 12-month pilot starting in H2 2027 is a limited practical exercise with 36 payment service providers to test technical functionality, operational processes, and user experience and gather evidence for future decisions. Actual issuance requires completion of the EU legislative process and a separate formal decision. [1][2][7]

### Which payment service providers were selected for the digital euro pilot?

On July 14, 2026, the ECB selected 36 payment service providers from more than 50 applicants across the euro area. Published coverage notes participants including Revolut, Stripe, UniCredit, Deutsche Bank, and SumUp. [1][5][6]

### Is the digital euro already issued or open for public investment?

No. As of November 27, 2026, no final issuance decision or public launch date has been confirmed, and the ECB warns the public against scams claiming to offer digital euro investments or early participation. [2][7]
