# ChatGPT

> ChatGPT is a conversational AI application developed by OpenAI and launched on November 30, 2022. As of 2026-11-23, it combines chat, web search, ChatGPT Work for document and spreadsheet tasks, and the Codex coding agent across web, desktop, and mobile clients. OpenAI offers Free, Go, Plus, three-tier Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu plans.

ChatGPT is a conversational artificial intelligence software application developed by OpenAI that provides text chat, web search, voice interaction, image generation, document creation through ChatGPT Work, and coding assistance through Codex. OpenAI launched ChatGPT on November 30, 2022, as a free research preview built on a model in the GPT-3.5 series trained with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) on Azure AI supercomputing infrastructure. As of 2026-11-23, ChatGPT is offered on the web, macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android across individual Free, Go, Plus, and three-tier Pro subscriptions, as well as Business, Enterprise, and Edu organizational plans. OpenAI states that customer workspace data in ChatGPT Business is excluded from model training under its Business Terms and Enterprise Privacy policies.

**Also known as:** ChatGPT Work, ChatGPT Search, ChatGPT Business, ChatGPT Enterprise

## ChatGPT launch, training background, and core product pillars

ChatGPT was introduced by [OpenAI](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/openai) on November 30, 2022, as a free research preview built for conversational interaction.[2] OpenAI described the initial release as a sibling model to InstructGPT, fine-tuned from a model in the GPT-3.5 series that had finished training in early 2022 on Azure AI supercomputing infrastructure.[2] In its Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, filed on [SEC EDGAR](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/sec-edgar) under CIK 0000789019, Microsoft discloses a long-term strategic partnership with OpenAI.[1]

OpenAI trained the initial ChatGPT model using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).[2] Human AI trainers first produced supervised fine-tuning dialogues by writing both user and assistant turns, which OpenAI combined with the InstructGPT dataset converted into dialogue format.[2] OpenAI then collected ranked comparisons of alternative model completions to train a reward model and ran iterative fine-tuning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).[2] At launch, OpenAI noted five limitations: producing plausible-sounding but incorrect or nonsensical answers, sensitivity to prompt phrasing, verbose responses with repeated boilerplate statements, guessing intent on ambiguous inputs, and occasional unsafe or biased outputs monitored through its Moderation API.[2]

As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI organizes ChatGPT around three core product areas: Chat for everyday questions, writing, web search, voice conversation, and image generation; ChatGPT Work for creating and editing documents, slide decks, spreadsheets, charts, and PDFs alongside connected tools such as Gmail and Slack; and Codex for software engineering tasks spanning planning, writing, testing, reviewing, and shipping code.[3] ChatGPT runs on the web, iOS, and Android, and the combined Chat, Work, and Codex experience is available to all plans on macOS and Windows desktop, and to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu plans on web and mobile.[3][4][9] Additional product areas listed on the ChatGPT overview page as of 2026-11-23 include Deep Research, Voice, Study Mode, Plugins, Sites, Space, Dots, Health, and Finance.[3]

## ChatGPT individual plans, models, and pricing

ChatGPT offers four individual subscription tiers as of 2026-11-23: Free, Go, Plus, and Pro, with Pro split into three monthly usage tiers.[4][6] OpenAI first introduced ChatGPT Go in India in August 2025, expanded it to 170 additional countries, and released it worldwide on January 16, 2026.[5] In the January 16, 2026 announcement, OpenAI stated US prices of $8 per month for ChatGPT Go (with localized pricing in some markets), $20 per month for ChatGPT Plus, and $200 per month for ChatGPT Pro.[5] In the same announcement, OpenAI stated that it planned to begin testing advertisements in the Free tier and ChatGPT Go in the United States, while keeping Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise free of advertisements.[5] As of 2026-11-23, the ChatGPT pricing page displays an advertisement notice on the Go plan.[4]

As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI offers ChatGPT Pro in three tiers billed on a monthly basis without an annual billing option: Pro 100 at $100 per month, Pro 200 at $200 per month, and Pro 500 at $500 per month.[6] Pro 200 includes more usage than Pro 100, and Pro 500 provides the highest included allowance and is the only Pro tier with Ultrafast mode for GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol.[6] Compared with Standard mode for the same model, Fast mode consumes included allowance at 2.5 times the rate, and Ultrafast consumes included allowance at 8 times the rate before drawing from purchased credits at 6 times the Standard credit rate.[6] Pro subscribers can purchase credits for eligible usage beyond included allowances in Codex, ChatGPT Work, ChatGPT for Word, ChatGPT for Excel, and ChatGPT for PowerPoint.[6]

Across individual plans, OpenAI states that unlimited access is subject to abuse guardrails, and the ChatGPT Pro documentation states that sharing account credentials, reselling access, and automatically extracting data are prohibited under the Terms of Use.[4][6] As of 2026-11-23, the ChatGPT pricing page also lists a Rosalind Academic Plan for life sciences research.[4]

## ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, Edu, and credit-based pricing

ChatGPT Business is OpenAI's self-serve shared workspace plan for organizations, renamed from ChatGPT Team on August 29, 2025.[7] Organizations can start a ChatGPT Business workspace with one paid seat, manage members and roles through Workspace settings or the Admin Console, and assign or reassign seat capacity across users.[7] As of 2026-11-23, ChatGPT Business offers Standard seats and Premium seats, allows workspaces to mix both seat types, and supports workspace credit purchases to extend usage beyond included rate limits.[7] Workspaces created before June 24, 2026, also had access to a legacy Codex seat type.[7]

| Seat type | Monthly billing (USD) | Annual billing (USD) | Included access (as of 2026-11-23) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard seat | $25 per user per month | $20 per user per month | ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, Codex, GPTs, Projects, Apps, Company Knowledge, ChatGPT Agent, and Deep Research |
| Premium seat | $125 per user per month | $100 per user per month | Standard seat access with higher ChatGPT Work and Codex usage limits and no five-hour usage limit |

ChatGPT Enterprise, ChatGPT Edu, Health, Gov, and ChatGPT for Teachers serve larger or specialized deployments, with Enterprise provided as a sales-led contracted offering for organizations requiring invoice billing, purchase orders, wire or ACH transfers, net payment terms, Zero Data Retention, or Business Associate Agreements (BAAs).[7][8] ChatGPT subscriptions are separate from the OpenAI API platform, and API consumption is billed independently.[7][8]

For ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, Edu, Health, Gov, and ChatGPT for Teachers workspaces on credit-based flexible pricing, OpenAI publishes a credit rate card covering fixed per-unit Chat charges and token-based Work and Codex charges.[8] Enterprise and Edu workspaces on credit-based flexible pricing scale usage with credits without fixed rate limits, and ChatGPT Edu includes a daily image usage budget plus 5 Deep Research queries per rolling 24-hour period before consuming credits.[8] As of 2026-11-23, the credit rate card lists the following per-unit Chat and feature rates: Instant messages are unlimited subject to abuse guardrails; GPT-6 (GPT-6 Sol) and GPT-5.6 Sol cost 10 credits per message; GPT-5.5 Rosalind Research and Discovery (available on select Enterprise plans) cost 20 credits per message; GPT-6 Pro (powered by GPT-6 Astra) and GPT-5.6 Sol Pro cost 50 credits per message; Agent mode costs 30 credits per message; Deep Research costs 50 credits per task; and Voice costs 1.25 credits per minute.[8]

## ChatGPT Search and publisher partnerships

ChatGPT Search is the integrated web search capability inside ChatGPT, launched by OpenAI on October 31, 2024, following the SearchGPT prototype announced on July 25, 2024.[10] At launch on October 31, 2024, OpenAI rolled out search on `chatgpt.com` and desktop and mobile apps to ChatGPT Plus, Team, and SearchGPT waitlist users, alongside a Chrome extension for searching from the browser address bar.[10] OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Search to all logged-in users in supported regions on December 16, 2024, and opened access to everyone in supported regions without signup on February 5, 2025.[10] As of 2026-11-23, Search is included across Free, Go, Plus, and Pro plans.[4]

ChatGPT decides automatically whether to search the web based on the user's question, and users can also trigger a web search manually through the web search icon.[10] Search responses include inline links to sources such as news articles and blog posts, along with a Sources button that opens a sidebar listing referenced pages.[10] OpenAI also built structured visual layouts with news and data providers for weather, stocks, sports, news, and maps.[10]

OpenAI stated that the initial ChatGPT Search model was a fine-tuned version of GPT-4o post-trained using synthetic data generation techniques, including distilling outputs from OpenAI o1-preview, and that ChatGPT Search combined third-party search providers with direct partner content.[10] At launch, OpenAI named fourteen global publisher partners: Associated Press, Axel Springer, Condé Nast, Dotdash Meredith, Financial Times, GEDI, Hearst, Le Monde, News Corp, Prisa (El País), Reuters, The Atlantic, Time, and Vox Media, while stating that any website or publisher could choose to appear in ChatGPT Search.[10]

## ChatGPT Work, Codex integration, and open agent protocols

ChatGPT includes ChatGPT Work and the Codex coding agent across Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Edu, and Enterprise plans, where both products share pricing, credits, and usage limits under the ChatGPT subscription.[9] Within the ChatGPT ecosystem, Codex operates across the ChatGPT desktop app, Codex CLI, Codex IDE extension, and Codex Cloud, covering local tasks, cloud tasks, scheduled automations, code review, and delegated workers.[8][9] ChatGPT Work produces and edits deliverables such as documents, slide decks, spreadsheets, charts, and PDFs, and connects to external work tools including Gmail and Slack.[3]

Under credit-based flexible pricing for Business, Enterprise, and Edu workspaces, ChatGPT Work and Codex usage is calculated per 1 million input tokens, cached input tokens, and output tokens, with no charge for cache writes and a 2x credit rate for Fast mode.[8] OpenAI states that GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-6.2 Sol, GPT-6.1 Terra, GPT-6.1 Sol, and GPT-6 Luna are available in ChatGPT Work and Codex and are excluded from regular Chat, that a typical Codex task using GPT-6.2 Sol consumes between 2 and 15 credits, and that Enterprise Codex usage signed in with ChatGPT carries no 272K-input-token pricing threshold.[8] As of 2026-11-23, the Standard-mode credit rate card lists the following token rates for Work and Codex.[8]

ChatGPT and Codex also participate in open agent standards hosted by the Linux Foundation.[12] On December 9, 2025, the Linux Foundation announced the formation of the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) with OpenAI as a Platinum member alongside Amazon Web Services, Anthropic, Block, Bloomberg, Cloudflare, Google, and Microsoft.[12] The announcement notes that ChatGPT adopted Anthropic's [Model Context Protocol](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/model-context-protocol) (MCP) for connecting models to tools, data, and applications, and that OpenAI contributed its Markdown-based `AGENTS.md` convention (released in August 2025 and adopted by more than 60,000 open source projects and agent frameworks including Codex), alongside ACP, Codex CLI, the Agents SDK, and the Apps SDK.[12]

## ChatGPT data training policy, safety controls, and developer platform documentation

ChatGPT applies distinct data governance terms to organizational workspaces and individual accounts.[7][3] In the ChatGPT Business overview documentation, OpenAI states that ChatGPT Business adheres to its Business Terms and Enterprise Privacy policies and that OpenAI does not train models on customer workspace data.[7] Organizations requiring Zero Data Retention or Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) use contracted offerings such as ChatGPT Enterprise.[7] For specialized domains, OpenAI's rate card also documents Trusted Access approval programs, including Astra for Law, GPT-6 Rosalind Research for life sciences, and Daybreak (Trusted Access for Cyber) for cybersecurity models.[8]

For individual users, the ChatGPT overview page states that account holders control privacy choices regarding how their data is used and provides Parental Controls allowing parents to configure safeguards for teen users.[3]

OpenAI also maintains developer and web-ecosystem documentation tied to ChatGPT on `developers.openai.com` and `learn.chatgpt.com`.[9][11] As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI's ChatGPT developer documentation covers Plugins built on MCP servers, Sign in with ChatGPT, Commerce integrations, Ads measurement and Advertiser APIs, Workspace Agents, and Agent Access for allowing verified ChatGPT browser visits to websites, alongside an Overview of OpenAI Crawlers reference page.[11] Both `learn.chatgpt.com` and `developers.openai.com` publish an [llms.txt](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/llms-txt) documentation index and serve Markdown versions of documentation pages when `.md` is appended to a page URL.[9][11]

As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI's official supported countries and territories directory lists 208 countries and territories for access to OpenAI services, and states that accessing or offering access from outside the listed locations may result in account blocking or suspension.[13]

## Frequently asked questions

### When was ChatGPT launched and how was its first version trained?

OpenAI launched ChatGPT on November 30, 2022, as a free research preview fine-tuned from a GPT-3.5 series model on Azure AI supercomputing infrastructure. OpenAI trained it using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), combining supervised fine-tuning on human trainer conversations with reward modeling and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). [2]

### What individual ChatGPT subscription plans and Pro tiers are available?

As of 2026-11-23, ChatGPT offers four individual tiers: Free, Go, Plus, and Pro. OpenAI announced US monthly pricing of $8 for Go and $20 for Plus on January 16, 2026, and as of 2026-11-23 offers Pro in three monthly-billed tiers: Pro 100 at $100 per month, Pro 200 at $200 per month, and Pro 500 at $500 per month, with Ultrafast mode included on Pro 500. [4][5][6]

### How much does ChatGPT Business cost per seat?

As of 2026-11-23, ChatGPT Business Standard seats cost $20 per user per month when billed annually or $25 per user per month when billed monthly, and Premium seats cost $100 per user per month billed annually or $125 per user per month billed monthly. Workspaces can start with one paid seat, mix Standard and Premium seats, and purchase workspace credits for additional usage. [7]

### Does OpenAI train models on ChatGPT Business workspace data?

No. As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI's ChatGPT Business overview states that ChatGPT Business follows OpenAI's Business Terms and Enterprise Privacy policies and that OpenAI does not train models on customer workspace data. Organizations requiring Zero Data Retention or Business Associate Agreements use a contracted offering such as ChatGPT Enterprise. [7]

### Is ChatGPT Search free to use without an account?

Yes. After launching ChatGPT Search to paid and waitlist users on October 31, 2024, and to all logged-in users on December 16, 2024, OpenAI opened ChatGPT Search on February 5, 2025, to everyone in regions where ChatGPT is available with no signup required. [10]

### How are ChatGPT Work and Codex included in ChatGPT plans?

As of 2026-11-23, OpenAI includes ChatGPT Work and Codex across Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Edu, and Enterprise plans, where both products share pricing, credits, and usage limits. Free and Go plans provide limited desktop access, while Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu provide expanded access across desktop, web, and mobile, with optional credit purchases for eligible usage beyond included allowances. [3][4][9]
