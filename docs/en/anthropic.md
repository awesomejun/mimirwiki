# Anthropic

> Anthropic, PBC is a Delaware public benefit corporation headquartered in San Francisco, California, founded in 2021 to conduct artificial intelligence safety research and develop the Claude AI models. Its governance pairs the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which elects a board majority via Class T stock, with Founder LLC's Class F voting stock. Anthropic completed its Nasdaq IPO under ticker ANT in November 2026 at $83.00 per share.

Anthropic, PBC is a United States artificial intelligence safety and research company incorporated in Delaware as a public benefit corporation and headquartered in San Francisco, California. Founded in 2021 by Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, and five other co-founders, Anthropic develops the Claude family of artificial intelligence models and related developer tools under a charter purpose of the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. Its governance structure pairs public benefit corporation status with the Long-Term Benefit Trust, an independent Delaware purpose trust holding Class T common stock with authority to elect a majority of the board of directors, alongside Founder LLC's Class F common stock carrying 50.1% of general stockholder voting power. Anthropic completed its initial public offering on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under ticker symbol ANT in November 2026, selling 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83.00 per share including full exercise of the underwriters' option, alongside a $26.0 billion concurrent private placement of Class N stock.

## Anthropic public benefit governance and share structure

Anthropic, PBC is incorporated under Delaware law as a public benefit corporation with the stated charter purpose of the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.[2][4][12] Under Delaware public benefit corporation law, Anthropic is required to balance the pecuniary interests of its stockholders, the best interests of those materially affected by its conduct, and the specific public benefit purpose identified in its amended and restated certificate of incorporation.[2][4][12]

To pair this corporate form with board oversight, Anthropic announced the Long-Term Benefit Trust (LTBT) on September 19, 2023.[4] The LTBT is organized as a purpose trust under Delaware common law with the same purpose as Anthropic and is structured as an independent body of five financially disinterested trustees with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy, and social enterprise.[2][4][12] Anthropic stated that it expects the LTBT to address long-range matters such as evaluating models for catastrophic risks and maintaining high-level security while management handles ordinary commercial strategy.[4] As of 2026-11-23, Anthropic's company page lists four LTBT trustees: Dr. Ben Bernanke, Richard Fontaine, Neil Buddy Shah, and Dr. Christopher Stubbs, who joined on October 21, 2026.[3][4][14]

Anthropic's Form 424B4 final prospectus details five authorized classes of common stock (Class A, Class N, Class F, Class T, and Class E) and notes that Anthropic qualifies as a controlled company under Nasdaq Global Select Market corporate governance requirements.[12] The table below summarizes the verified voting, economic, and conversion rights documented in Form 424B4, Form S-1/A-3, and the LTBT announcement.[2][4][12]

## Anthropic Constitutional AI and safety research

Anthropic organizes its work across four operational areas, namely research, policy, product, and operations, and conducts technical research on interpretability, alignment, and the economic and societal impacts of artificial intelligence.[3] The company describes its technical objective as building AI systems that are reliable, interpretable, and steerable, applying research findings to its products, and publishing research results.[3]

On December 15, 2022, Yuntao Bai and 50 co-authors at Anthropic, including Saurav Kadavath, Sandipan Kundu, Amanda Askell, Dario Amodei, and Jared Kaplan, submitted the paper Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback (arXiv:2212.08073).[7] Constitutional AI trains a harmless AI assistant through self-improvement using a written list of rules or principles as the sole human oversight for harmlessness, without human labels identifying harmful outputs.[7]

- Supervised learning stage: the method samples responses from an initial model, generates self-critiques and revisions guided by the written principles, and fine-tunes the original model on the revised responses.[7]
- Reinforcement learning stage (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback, RLAIF): the method samples pairs of responses from the fine-tuned model, uses an AI model to evaluate which response better satisfies the principles, trains a preference model on the resulting dataset of AI preferences, and trains the assistant with reinforcement learning using that preference model as the reward signal.[7]

According to the paper, both the supervised learning and reinforcement learning stages can use chain-of-thought reasoning to improve human-judged performance and transparency in AI decision-making, producing an assistant that addresses harmful queries by explaining its objections while requiring far fewer human labels.[7]

## Anthropic Responsible Scaling Policy

Anthropic maintains a voluntary risk-governance framework called the Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) to address catastrophic risks from advanced AI systems, first taking effect on September 19, 2023.[6] As of the August 14, 2026 update to Anthropic's RSP page, the policy is at Version 3.4, effective July 8, 2026.[6]

Under the RSP, reaching designated capability thresholds requires upgrading model protections to higher safeguard standards, including the ASL-3 Security Standard and ASL-3 Deployment Standard outlined alongside Version 2.0 on October 15, 2024.[6] The planned ASL-3 Deployment Safeguards address potential misuse of chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) capabilities through four layers: access controls with enhanced due diligence for partners needing tailored safeguards, real-time prompt and completion classifiers with online interventions, asynchronous monitoring classifiers, and post-hoc jailbreak detection with rapid response procedures.[6]

In addition to the policy text, Anthropic published an external-facing Sabotage Risk Report for Claude Opus 4.6 on February 10, 2026.[6] Anthropic determined that Claude Opus 4.6 did not cross the AI R&D-4 capability threshold, while publishing the report to fulfill a commitment made during the launch of Claude Opus 4.5 to produce sabotage risk reports for future frontier models exceeding Opus 4.5.[6] Anthropic also released an RSP Noncompliance Reporting and Anti-Retaliation Policy internally in February 2026 and posted an updated public version on March 24, 2026, alongside Redacted Risk Reports in February 2026 and on August 14, 2026 (covering model risks through July 15, 2026).[6] In September and October 2026, Anthropic introduced the Life Sciences Verification Program (September 17, 2026) and an expanded Cyber Verification Program (October 6, 2026) providing qualifying researchers and security professionals access to models with tailored safeguards.[14]

## Anthropic products, open-source software, and the Model Context Protocol

Anthropic delivers its research through the [Claude](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/claude) family of AI models and related software products, including Claude.ai, the Claude console, the Claude Developer Platform, Claude Desktop apps, Claude for Work, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork.[3][5][8][9] Across Anthropic's announcements and primary disclosures through November 2026, released models include the Claude 3 family, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude Opus 4.5 and 4.6, Claude Mythos and Fable 5.1 (September 15, 2026), Claude Opus 5.5 (September 22, 2026), Claude Sonnet 5.5 (September 28, 2026), Claude Haiku 5.5 (October 7, 2026), and [Claude Fable 5.2](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/claude-fable-5-2) and Claude Mythos 5.2 (November 9, 2026).[5][6][10][14]

On November 25, 2024, Anthropic open-sourced the [Model Context Protocol](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/model-context-protocol) (MCP), an open standard created at Anthropic by David Soria Parra and Justin Spahr-Summers for two-way connections between AI assistants and data sources such as content repositories, business tools, and development environments.[5] MCP defines an architecture in which MCP servers expose data sources and MCP clients inside AI applications connect to those servers.[5] On August 27, 2026, Anthropic also opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a shared specification for AI agents to operate physical devices safely in scientific labs and advanced manufacturing.[14]

On December 9, 2025, Anthropic contributed MCP as a founding project to the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF) under the Linux Foundation, alongside Block's goose framework and [OpenAI](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/openai)'s AGENTS.md convention.[8] Anthropic joined the AAIF as a Platinum member alongside Amazon Web Services, Block, Bloomberg, [Cloudflare](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/cloudflare), Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI.[8] As of December 9, 2025, the Linux Foundation reported more than 10,000 published MCP servers and adoption across [Claude](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/claude), [Cursor](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/cursor), [Microsoft Copilot](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/microsoft-copilot), [Google Gemini](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/google-gemini), VS Code, and [ChatGPT](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/chatgpt).[8]

As of 2026-11-23, Anthropic's verified GitHub organization (`anthropics`, verified for the domain `anthropic.com`) lists 118 public repositories and 99.9k followers, with top languages including Python, Rust, Go, TypeScript, and C++.[9] Public repositories listed on the organization overview include `skills` (Agent Skills, approximately 199,000 stars), `claude-code` (a terminal-based agentic coding tool, 156,772 stars), `claude-cookbooks` (approximately 55,100 stars), `claude-plugins-official` (48,023 stars, Apache-2.0), `financial-services` (approximately 41,500 stars), `prompt-eng-interactive-tutorial` (approximately 39,700 stars), `knowledge-work-plugins` (open-source plugins for Claude Cowork, 36,731 stars, Apache-2.0), `claude-for-legal` (10,075 stars, Apache-2.0), and `claude-code-action` (10,055 stars, MIT).[9]

## Anthropic cloud infrastructure and strategic investments

Anthropic entered into a strategic collaboration and minority investment agreement with Amazon, which completed a total investment of $4 billion in Anthropic on March 27, 2024.[10] Amazon made an initial $1.25 billion investment in September 2023 and added $2.75 billion on March 27, 2024 for a minority ownership position.[10] Anthropic's Form S-1/A-3 filed on November 17, 2026 also references convertible instruments termed the Amazon Notes when reporting Anthropic's fully diluted share count as of June 30, 2026.[2]

Under the strategic agreement reported by Amazon, Anthropic uses Amazon Web Services (AWS) as its primary cloud provider for core workloads including safety research and future foundation model development, and committed to using AWS Trainium and Inferentia chips to build, train, and deploy future models.[10] Anthropic also made a long-term commitment to provide AWS customers access to future generations of its foundation models on Amazon Bedrock, where the Claude 3 model family became available in March 2024.[10] Alongside the cloud collaboration, AWS, Anthropic, and Accenture announced a joint effort to support enterprise generative AI deployments in regulated sectors including healthcare, the public sector, banking, and insurance.[10]

During its November 2026 IPO, Anthropic also expanded strategic capital and compute-ecosystem relationships through a $26.0 billion concurrent private placement of 313,253,007 Class N shares at $83.00 per share with Advanced Micro Devices, Inc., Broadcom Inc., Google LLC (funding a portion of its previously committed investment obligations under the Google 2026 NPA), Micron Technology, Inc., Microsoft Corporation, [NVIDIA](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/nvidia) Corporation, and TSMC Partners, Ltd.[11][12] The concurrent private placement closed on November 20, 2026 alongside the IPO.[14]
