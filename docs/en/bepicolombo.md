# BepiColombo

> BepiColombo is a joint ESA-JAXA mission that entered Mercury orbit on 2026-11-21 UTC after an eight-year cruise. ESA's MPO studies the planet itself while JAXA's Mio investigates Mercury's magnetosphere; despite an early thruster cut-off during orbit insertion, both orbiters are healthy and Mio's separation remains scheduled for 2026-12-10 JST.

BepiColombo is a joint Mercury exploration mission by the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) carrying two science orbiters: ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio). Launched on 2018-10-20 UTC, BepiColombo entered Mercury orbit on 2026-11-21 UTC (2026-11-22 JST), becoming the second mission ever to orbit Mercury, with JAXA confirming on 2026-11-26 that Mio's planned separation remains scheduled for 2026-12-10 JST.

## BepiColombo dual-orbiter design and scientific roles

BepiColombo is a joint international mission by the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA) to investigate Mercury, the innermost and least explored planet in the inner Solar System.[2][3] Launched on October 20, 2018 (01:45 GMT) aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from Europe's Spaceport, the composite spacecraft had a launch mass of 4,100 kg, including 1,400 kg of propellant.[3]

The mission combines two dedicated science spacecraft carried together during the eight-year interplanetary cruise: ESA's 1,230 kg Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO, carrying 85 kg of science payload) and JAXA's 255 kg Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio, carrying 45 kg of science payload), transported by the Mercury Transport Module (MTM).[2][3] Together they are designed to study Mercury's planetary properties and its magnetosphere and plasma environment.[2][4]

## BepiColombo November 2026 Mercury orbit insertion

BepiColombo achieved Mercury orbit insertion (MOI) starting at 18:24 UTC on November 21, 2026 (November 22, 2026 JST), marking the first visit to Mercury orbit by a European or Japanese spacecraft and the second mission ever to orbit Mercury.[1][2][5]

When communications resumed after a scheduled loss of contact, mission controllers at ESA's European Space Operations Centre (ESOC) in Darmstadt, Germany, confirmed that the combined spacecraft had entered safe mode.[1][2] JAXA's Institute of Space and Astronautical Science (ISAS) reported on November 26, 2026, that an onboard software issue on the MPO caused the thruster firing during orbit insertion to terminate earlier than planned, leaving the spacecraft in a higher-altitude orbit than originally targeted.[2]

## BepiColombo spacecraft health and Mio separation schedule

BepiColombo remained healthy after entering Mercury orbit despite the early burn cut-off, according to both ESA and JAXA.[1][2] JAXA ISAS stated on November 26, 2026, that the higher-than-planned orbit will be corrected through a series of orbit-control maneuvers designed to gradually lower altitude.[2]

Crucially, JAXA confirmed that the planned separation and operation of the Mio orbiter remains on schedule and is expected to take place on December 10, 2026 JST.[2]

## BepiColombo upcoming milestones and unverified status

BepiColombo's two orbiters remain connected as of November 26, 2026, ahead of their scheduled December separation.[2] Actual orbiter separation on December 10 JST, subsequent orbit-lowering burns, and the start of full dual-orbiter science observations have not yet occurred and remain subject to mission execution.[2][6]

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the two orbiters on BepiColombo and what do they study?

BepiColombo carries ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO, 1,230 kg) to study Mercury's planetary characteristics and JAXA's Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio, 255 kg) to study Mercury's magnetosphere and plasma environment. [2][3]

### When did BepiColombo enter Mercury orbit?

BepiColombo achieved Mercury orbit insertion starting at 18:24 UTC on November 21, 2026 (November 22, 2026 JST), after an eight-year journey from its October 20, 2018 launch. [1][2][3]

### What happened during BepiColombo's Mercury orbit insertion?

An onboard software issue on ESA's MPO caused the thruster burn to end earlier than planned, placing the combined spacecraft into safe mode and a higher-altitude orbit, though both agencies confirmed the spacecraft is healthy. [1][2]

### When is JAXA's Mio orbiter scheduled to separate?

JAXA ISAS announced on November 26, 2026, that Mio's separation remains on schedule for December 10, 2026 JST. [2]
