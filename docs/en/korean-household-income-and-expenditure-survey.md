# Household Income and Expenditure Survey (South Korea)

> The Household Income and Expenditure Survey is South Korea's quarterly national survey of monthly household income, spending, and income distribution covering all households of one or more persons. In its November 26, 2026 third-quarter report, nominal monthly household income rose 0.8% year on year to 5,480,000 KRW while real income fell 2.1%, and disposable income declined 1.7% to 4,306,000 KRW.

The Household Income and Expenditure Survey (South Korea) is a quarterly national statistical survey compiled by the Ministry of Data and Statistics (formerly Statistics Korea) that measures monthly average household income, consumption expenditure, non-consumption expenditure, and income distribution across all households of one or more persons, including single-person and agricultural-forestry-fishery households. The survey reports both nominal figures and inflation-adjusted real growth rates, along with the equivalized disposable income quintile share ratio. In the third-quarter 2026 release published on November 26, 2026, monthly average household income reached 5,480,000 KRW, up 0.8% nominally year on year but down 2.1% in real terms. Monthly average consumption expenditure rose 2.9% nominally to 3,030,000 KRW while real consumption expenditure was unchanged at 0.0%.

**Also known as:** Korean Household Income and Expenditure Survey, 가계동향조사

## Household Income and Expenditure Survey scope and indicators

The Household Income and Expenditure Survey (South Korea) reports quarterly monthly averages per household across all households of one or more persons nationwide, including single-person households and agricultural, forestry, and fishery households.[1] Year-on-year growth rates compare each quarter with the same quarter of the preceding year.[1]

The survey tracks total household income broken down into earned income, business income, and transfer income (public and private), alongside 12 categories of consumption expenditure and non-consumption expenditure such as taxes, interest payments, pension contributions, and inter-household transfers.[1][2] By subtracting non-consumption expenditure from total income, the survey derives disposable income, household surplus (disposable income minus consumption expenditure), and the average propensity to consume (consumption expenditure as a share of disposable income).[1] Income distribution is measured using the equivalized disposable income quintile share ratio, which compares the top 20% income group (5th quintile) with the bottom 20% (1st quintile).[1][5]

## Household Income and Expenditure Survey nominal versus real income

The Household Income and Expenditure Survey reports both nominal changes and inflation-adjusted real changes, which can move in opposite directions when consumer price inflation exceeds nominal income growth.[1][6] In the third quarter of 2026, monthly average household income increased 0.8% nominally from a year earlier to 5,480,000 KRW, whereas real income after accounting for inflation declined 2.1%.[1]

According to the November 26, 2026 briefing by the Ministry of Data and Statistics, current income rose 0.4% to 5,355,000 KRW.[1] Earned income grew 3.6% to 3,487,000 KRW and private transfer income increased 20.4%, whereas business income fell 1.4% to 975,000 KRW and total transfer income dropped 8.5% to 845,000 KRW.[1] Public transfer income declined 15.4% year on year, which Yonhap News and Maeil Business Newspaper attributed to the base effect of livelihood recovery consumption coupons distributed in the third quarter of 2025.[2][6]

## Household Income and Expenditure Survey Q3 2026 spending and balance

In the third quarter of 2026, monthly average consumption expenditure rose 2.9% nominally year on year to 3,030,000 KRW, marking 23 consecutive quarters of nominal growth, while inflation-adjusted real consumption expenditure stayed flat at 0.0%.[1][2] Nominal spending increased in transportation (+9.6%, to 357,000 KRW), housing, water, electricity, and fuel (+4.9%, to 351,000 KRW), health (+4.1%, to 238,000 KRW), food and non-alcoholic beverages (+3.3%, to 472,000 KRW), and information and communications (+7.5%, to 181,000 KRW), while declining in clothing and footwear (-4.6%, to 113,000 KRW), alcoholic beverages and tobacco (-4.0%, to 39,000 KRW), and education (-2.4%, to 231,000 KRW).[1] In real terms, Yonhap News reported declines in clothing and footwear (-7.2%), alcoholic beverages and tobacco (-4.2%), recreation and culture (-4.0%), and education (-3.7%).[2]

Non-consumption expenditure rose 11.0% year on year to 1,174,000 KRW, led by inter-household transfers (+30.0%), interest expenses (+11.0%), regular taxes (+8.0%), and pension contributions (+7.8%).[1][2] As a result, monthly disposable income fell 1.7% to 4,306,000 KRW, its first decrease in 13 quarters since Q2 2023, while monthly household surplus dropped 11.2% to 1,276,000 KRW and the average propensity to consume rose 3.2 percentage points to 70.4%.[1][2]

## Household Income and Expenditure Survey Q3 2026 quintile distribution

The Household Income and Expenditure Survey for the third quarter of 2026 showed a widening gap between lower-income and higher-income households.[1][5] The equivalized disposable income quintile share ratio stood at 5.39 in Q3 2026.[1]

Monthly average income for the bottom 20% (1st quintile) fell 2.7% year on year to 1,277,000 KRW, with disposable income down 5.4% to 1,065,000 KRW and an average propensity to consume of 132.3% (+9.2 percentage points).[1] By contrast, monthly average income for the top 20% (5th quintile) increased 1.4% to 11,746,000 KRW, while disposable income edged down 0.7% to 8,999,000 KRW and average propensity to consume reached 59.0% (+4.1 percentage points).[1] Trends for the fourth quarter of 2026 and detailed breakdowns by household type have not yet been released as of November 26, 2026.[1]

## Frequently asked questions

### What does South Korea's Household Income and Expenditure Survey measure?

It is a quarterly national survey by the Ministry of Data and Statistics that measures monthly average household income, 12 categories of consumption expenditure, non-consumption expenditure, disposable income, household surplus, and income distribution across all households of one or more persons. [1]

### Why can real household income fall when average nominal income rises?

Real income adjusts nominal income for consumer price inflation. In Q3 2026, nominal monthly household income rose 0.8% year on year to 5,480,000 KRW, but because inflation exceeded that pace, real income declined 2.1%. [1][6]

### Why did disposable income decline in Q3 2026?

Disposable income is total income minus non-consumption expenditure. In Q3 2026, total nominal income grew only 0.8% due in part to a 15.4% drop in public transfer income following the prior year's consumption coupons, while non-consumption expenditure rose 11.0% to 1,174,000 KRW, pulling disposable income down 1.7% to 4,306,000 KRW. [1][2]

### What was the income gap between the 1st and 5th quintiles in Q3 2026?

In Q3 2026, the equivalized disposable income quintile share ratio was 5.39. Monthly average income fell 2.7% to 1,277,000 KRW for 1st-quintile households and rose 1.4% to 11,746,000 KRW for 5th-quintile households. [1]
