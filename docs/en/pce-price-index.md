# Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index

> The PCE Price Index measures price changes in U.S. consumer spending (distinct from nominal PCE spending growth). For October 2026 (released 2026-11-25), BEA reported headline PCE inflation of +0.3% month over month (+3.8% year over year) and core PCE inflation of +0.3% month over month (+3.3% year over year), while current-dollar PCE spending rose 0.3% and real PCE was flat (0.0%).

The Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Price Index is a monthly inflation gauge published by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) that tracks price changes in goods and services purchased by U.S. consumers, and serves as the Federal Reserve's benchmark for its 2 percent longer-run inflation objective. ## Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index definition and Federal Reserve role

The Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Price Index measures changes in the prices of goods and services purchased by consumers in the United States and is published monthly by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) inside the Personal Income and Outlays report.[2] BEA also publishes the PCE Price Index Excluding Food and Energy, known as the core PCE price index, to make underlying inflation trends clearer by removing two categories whose prices tend to fluctuate more sharply.[3]

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) has affirmed that an annual increase of 2 percent in the PCE price index is most consistent over the longer run with the Federal Reserve's statutory mandate for maximum employment and price stability.[5] According to the Federal Reserve, the FOMC uses the PCE price index largely because it covers a wide range of household spending, while also tracking other gauges such as the Consumer Price Index and Producer Price Index.[5]

## Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index vs. PCE spending growth

The Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index tracks price changes, whereas Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) measures the dollar value or inflation-adjusted volume of consumer spending.[1][2] In BEA's monthly Personal Income and Outlays report, current-dollar PCE reflects total nominal spending growth, while real PCE removes price changes measured by the PCE price index to show underlying consumption volume.[1]

For October 2026, BEA reported that current-dollar PCE increased by $63.8 billion (0.3 percent from September), while the PCE price index also rose 0.3 percent, leaving real PCE unchanged at 0.0 percent for the month.[1][4]

## Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index October 2026 results

The Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index for October 2026, released by BEA on November 25, 2026, showed headline inflation increasing 0.3 percent from September (slowing from 0.5 percent in September) and 3.8 percent from October 2025 (up from 3.7 percent in September).[1][2]

The core PCE price index excluding food and energy rose 0.3 percent in October (matching September's 0.3 percent monthly gain) and 3.3 percent from one year earlier, up from 3.1 percent in September and 3.0 percent in July and August.[1][3] In the same release, BEA reported that personal income rose $201.8 billion (0.7 percent), disposable personal income increased $200.5 billion (0.8 percent), personal saving stood at $1.00 trillion, and the personal saving rate was 4.1 percent.[1][4]
