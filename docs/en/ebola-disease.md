# Ebola disease

Ebola disease is a severe, often fatal viral illness caused by orthoebolaviruses, with three species (Ebola virus, Sudan virus, and Bundibugyo virus) known to cause large human outbreaks. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the average case fatality rate is around 50%, ranging from 25% to 90% in past outbreaks. In May 2026, a major Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), reaching 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths in the DRC as of 2026-11-17.

## Ebola disease overview, fatality rate, and clinical care

Ebola disease is a severe, often fatal viral illness in humans caused by orthoebolaviruses.[1] The World Health Organization (WHO) notes that three species have caused large outbreaks: Ebola virus (Zaire), Sudan virus, and Bundibugyo virus.[1] Across historical outbreaks, the average case fatality rate is approximately 50%, ranging between 25% and 90%.[1]

Approved vaccines and specific antiviral treatments currently exist only for disease caused by Ebola virus (Zaire), whereas no approved vaccine or specific treatment is yet available for Bundibugyo virus, although candidate medical countermeasures are under evaluation.[1][4] Early intensive supportive care with rehydration improves survival, and outbreak control relies on infection prevention and control (IPC), surveillance, contact tracing, laboratory testing, safe and dignified burials, and community engagement.[1][4]

## 2026 Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak in the DRC and neighboring countries

A Bundibugyo virus disease (BVD) outbreak was officially declared in northeastern Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) on May 15, 2026, with initial cases also confirmed across the border in Uganda and subsequent monitoring across multiple countries by WHO, ECDC, and the UK Health Security Agency.[4][5][6][8] By November 17, 2026, WHO Disease Outbreak News (2026-DON630) reported 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths (crude case fatality ratio 48.9%) across 68 health zones in eight DRC provinces, with active transmission ongoing in 48 health zones.[2]

Ituri and Nord-Kivu remained the most affected provinces, while Sud-Kivu and Sud-Ubangi had gone more than 42 days without new cases.[2] Community deaths outside treatment centers remained a major challenge: of 720 deaths reported in the DRC between October 28 and November 17, 452 occurred in communities compared with 268 in treatment centers.[2] Meanwhile, no new cases were reported in Kenya or Nigeria, and both countries entered the 42-day countdown toward declaring their outbreaks over.[2]

## Why confirmed case and death counts differ across situation reports

Published tallies for the 2026 outbreak vary across agencies and situation reports primarily because of differences in reporting cut-off dates and geographic scope.[2][3][7] For example, WHO DON625 (data as of November 3, 2026) reported 10,668 confirmed cases and 5,198 deaths across 67 DRC health zones, whereas WHO DON630 (data as of November 17, 2026) reported 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths across 68 DRC health zones.[2][3]

Scope differences also separate single-country from multi-country totals: for the same November 17, 2026 cut-off date, Contagion Live cited U.S. CDC figures of 11,645 total confirmed cases and 5,683 deaths across the multi-country outbreak, of which the DRC accounted for 11,613 cases and 5,678 deaths (marking the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record).[7] Later national situation reports published after November 17 could not be directly opened during this review.

## Outbreak response challenges and open uncertainties

WHO and Doctors Without Borders (MSF) report that the 2026 BVD outbreak is occurring amid conflict, insecurity, displacement, high population movement, and limited access to healthcare in northeastern DRC, which hinder early case detection and timely referral to treatment centers.[2][4][8] Contagion Live noted that no cases have been reported in the United States and that risk to the U.S. general public and travelers remains low.[7]

The trajectory of transmission in active DRC health zones, the clinical efficacy of investigational Bundibugyo countermeasures, and case counts after November 17, 2026 remain unverified as of November 26, 2026.[2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What causes Ebola disease and what is its typical fatality rate?

Ebola disease is caused by orthoebolaviruses, with Ebola virus, Sudan virus, and Bundibugyo virus responsible for large outbreaks. According to WHO, the average case fatality rate is around 50% (historically 25% to 90%). [1]

### Is there an approved vaccine or specific treatment for Bundibugyo virus disease?

No. WHO states that approved vaccines and treatments currently exist only for Ebola virus (Zaire); for Bundibugyo virus, candidates are under development and early intensive supportive care with rehydration is the primary clinical intervention. [1][4]

### What is the scale of the 2026 Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak?

As of November 17, 2026, WHO reported 11,613 confirmed cases and 5,678 deaths (48.9% CFR) across 68 health zones in 8 DRC provinces, while CDC figures cited by Contagion Live placed the multi-country total at 11,645 confirmed cases and 5,683 deaths. [2][7]

### Why do confirmed case and death counts differ between situation reports?

Counts differ because agencies use different data cut-off dates (for example, November 3 vs. November 17 in WHO DON625 and DON630) and different geographic scopes (DRC-only figures of 11,613 cases vs. multi-country totals of 11,645 cases). [2][3][7]
