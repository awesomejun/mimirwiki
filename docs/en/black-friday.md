## Black Friday overview and 2026 consumer demand

Black Friday is one of the highest-volume retail shopping days of the year in both brick-and-mortar stores and online, taking place on the Friday after [Thanksgiving Day](https://mimirwiki.com/wiki/thanksgiving-day-us) (2026-11-27 in 2026).[1][3] In its 2026-11-19 annual survey conducted with Prosper Insights & Analytics, the National Retail Federation (NRF) estimated that 182.7 million consumers plan to shop during the 2026 Thanksgiving weekend, down slightly from 186.9 million in 2025.[2]

Black Friday remains the most popular day of the weekend, with 69% (126 million) of holiday weekend shoppers planning to shop on that day.[2] NRF Vice President of Industry and Consumer Insights Allison Zeller noted that holiday shopping continues to be pulled forward, with 60% of consumers starting by the beginning of November compared with 56% before the pandemic.[2]

## Black Friday and 2026 online holiday sales forecast

Black Friday is a central driver of U.S. e-commerce growth during the November-December season. On 2026-09-28, Adobe Analytics forecast a record $275.1 billion in U.S. online holiday spending between 2026-11-01 and 2026-12-31, up 6.7% year over year, based on over 1 trillion visits to U.S. retail sites across 100 million SKUs and 18 product categories.[6]

Adobe projected that Cyber Monday will remain the single largest online shopping day of the season at over $15 billion, while Black Friday will see stronger growth due to earlier discounts of up to 30% off listed prices.[6] Actual realized 2026 Black Friday shopper turnout and final sales totals are not yet available as of 2026-11-25.[2][6]

## Black Friday shopping and cybersecurity guidance

Black Friday's high transaction volume also draws federal consumer security warnings. In a U.S. Census Bureau holiday feature quoting the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA), CISA advised shoppers to protect personal and payment information both in physical stores and online by using strong passwords, updating software, avoiding suspicious links, and enabling multi-factor authentication.[1]
