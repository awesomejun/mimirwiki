# Wind Advisory

> A Wind Advisory ('Take Action!') warns of strong winds that can make driving high-profile vehicles difficult and blow around loose outdoor items, whereas a High Wind Warning applies to stronger, potentially damaging winds (commonly 40+ mph sustained or 58+ mph gusts).

A Wind Advisory is an official public weather alert issued by local U.S. National Weather Service (NWS) forecast offices when strong winds are occurring or expected, but are not strong enough to warrant a High Wind Warning. Specific wind-speed thresholds vary by local NWS office criteria, typically around 30 to 39 mph sustained winds or gusts of 45 to 57 mph.

**Also known as:** NWS Wind Advisory

## Wind Advisory definition and local criteria

A Wind Advisory is issued by the National Weather Service when strong winds are occurring or expected, but do not reach the threshold of a High Wind Warning.[1] NWS national guidance specifies that local forecast offices issue Wind Advisories based on local criteria rather than a single nationwide speed cutoff.[1]

For example, NWS Des Moines defines a Wind Advisory as sustained winds of 30 mph for one hour and/or frequent gusts of at least 45 mph occurring or expected within 36 hours, while NWS Spokane defines a valley Wind Advisory as sustained winds of 30 to 39 mph and/or gusts of 45 to 57 mph (and does not issue Wind Advisories for mountain locations).[2][3]

## Wind Advisory vs. High Wind Warning and High Wind Watch

A Wind Advisory sits below a High Wind Warning in severity within the NWS wind alert hierarchy.[1][2] Under NWS Des Moines criteria, a High Wind Warning is issued when sustained winds of 40 mph for one hour and/or frequent gusts of at least 58 mph are occurring or expected within 36 hours, while a High Wind Watch signals those speeds are expected within 12 to 48 hours.[2]

Local terrain also changes warning thresholds: NWS Spokane sets High Wind Warning criteria at 40 mph sustained or 58 mph gusts in lower elevations, and 50 mph sustained or 75 mph gusts in mountain locations.[3] While a Wind Advisory warns mainly of hazardous driving for high-profile vehicles and unsecured outdoor items, a High Wind Warning warns of potential damage to trees, power lines, and small structures.[2]

## Wind Advisory example in the November 25, 2026 NWS New York storm bulletins

Wind Advisory and High Wind Warning alerts can be issued simultaneously for neighboring areas during the same storm system.[5][6] On November 25, 2026, the NWS New York NY Area Forecast Discussion (8:31 PM EST) reported that the Wind Advisory had been expanded into Orange and Putnam counties ahead of a Thanksgiving-into-Friday storm system bringing rain and strong to damaging winds.[5]

In the same storm, the NWS New York Zone Forecast Product (7:57 PM EST, November 25, 2026) placed Manhattan under a High Wind Warning from 10 AM Thursday to 1 AM EST Friday, forecasting northeast winds of 20 to 30 mph with gusts up to 45 mph increasing to 30 to 35 mph with gusts up to 55 mph on Thanksgiving afternoon.[6] Observed peak gusts after the forecast and subsequent advisory updates depend on actual storm evolution after November 25, 2026.[5]

## Wind Advisory safety actions

Wind Advisory safety guidance from the NWS tells the public to secure loose outdoor objects and use extra caution when driving, especially in high-profile vehicles.[1][2] When alerts escalate to a High Wind Warning, NWS advises postponing unnecessary driving and taking shelter inside a sturdy structure away from falling debris and downed power lines.[1][2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a Wind Advisory and a High Wind Warning?

A Wind Advisory is issued for strong winds below warning strength (commonly 30-39 mph sustained or 45-57 mph gusts), whereas a High Wind Warning is issued for stronger, potentially damaging winds (commonly 40+ mph sustained or 58+ mph gusts). [1][2][3]

### Does every U.S. region use the exact same wind speed threshold for a Wind Advisory?

No. The National Weather Service states that local forecast offices issue Wind Advisories based on local criteria, which can differ by terrain such as valleys versus mountains. [1][3]

### What precautions should people take during a Wind Advisory?

NWS recommends securing small or loose outdoor objects so they are not blown around and exercising extra caution while driving, particularly in high-profile vehicles. [1][2]

### Can a Wind Advisory and a High Wind Warning be active in nearby counties at the same time?

Yes. For example, on November 25, 2026, NWS New York expanded a Wind Advisory into Orange and Putnam counties while placing Manhattan under a High Wind Warning for the same Thanksgiving storm. [5][6]
