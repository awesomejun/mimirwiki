# ESCAPADE

> ESCAPADE is a NASA and UC Berkeley twin-spacecraft mission (Blue and Gold) studying how the solar wind strips Mars's atmosphere. Launched on 2025-11-13 aboard Blue Origin's New Glenn, the Rocket Lab-built orbiters completed powered Earth flybys on 2026-11-07 and 2026-11-09, with NASA releasing parting Earth images on 2026-11-23 ahead of a targeted September 2027 Mars arrival.

ESCAPADE (Escape and Plasma Acceleration and Dynamics Explorers) is a NASA heliophysics mission led by the University of California, Berkeley Space Sciences Laboratory that uses twin orbiters named Blue and Gold to study how the solar wind interacts with Mars's magnetic environment and drives atmospheric loss. Funded by NASA's Heliophysics Division under the SIMPLEx program with spacecraft built by Rocket Lab, ESCAPADE launched aboard a Blue Origin New Glenn rocket on 2025-11-13. After spending about a year in an Earth-proximity loiter orbit around Earth-Sun Lagrange point 2, Blue and Gold completed powered Earth flybys on 2026-11-07 and 2026-11-09 and are en route to a targeted Mars arrival in September 2027.

**Also known as:** Escape and Plasma Acceleration and Dynamics Explorers, EscaPADE

## ESCAPADE mission overview and partners

ESCAPADE (Escape and Plasma Acceleration and Dynamics Explorers) is a NASA heliophysics mission designed to coordinate two identical spacecraft, Blue and Gold, in orbit around Mars.[1][4][5] NASA describes ESCAPADE as the first coordinated multi-spacecraft orbital science mission to Mars.[4] The mission is part of NASA's Small Innovative Missions for Planetary Exploration (SIMPLEx) program and is funded by NASA's Heliophysics Division.[1][4]

The University of California, Berkeley Space Sciences Laboratory leads and operates ESCAPADE, with Robert Lillis serving as principal investigator.[2][5] Rocket Lab built the twin spacecraft, Advanced Space provided launch strategies, trajectory design, mission design, and navigation, with a trajectory design that the company credits to its co-founder and chief technology officer Jeffrey Parker, and Northern Arizona University provided the Visible and Infrared Observation System (VIS) camera.[1][4][7] Additional key partners named by NASA include NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University, and Blue Origin.[1] NASA's Launch Services Program at Kennedy Space Center procured launch services under the Venture-class Acquisition of Dedicated and Rideshare (VADR) contract.[4]

## ESCAPADE launch and Earth-proximity loiter orbit

ESCAPADE launched on 2025-11-13 at 3:55 p.m. EST aboard a Blue Origin New Glenn rocket from Launch Complex 36 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida.[3] Both spacecraft deployed from the New Glenn second stage, and ground controllers established communications with both probes by 10:35 p.m. EST the same day.[3][4]

Because Earth and Mars were on opposite sides of the Sun at launch, ESCAPADE did not fly directly to Mars.[2] Instead, both spacecraft entered an Earth-proximity, or loiter, orbit following a kidney-bean-shaped path around Earth-Sun Lagrange point 2 (L2), about one million miles from Earth, for roughly one year.[2][4] During this loiter phase, the twin spacecraft collected science observations while crossing Earth's magnetotail multiple times.[2]

## ESCAPADE powered Earth flybys and parting Earth images

ESCAPADE executed two powered Earth flybys in November 2026, combining Earth's gravity assist with sustained engine burns to take advantage of the Oberth effect and enter a Mars-bound trajectory.[2][4] NASA stated that this was the first time the agency used a powered Earth flyby to propel a spacecraft toward Mars.[2]

- **Blue (first spacecraft)**: Completed its close approach and trans-Mars injection burn lasting more than 9 minutes starting at 5 a.m. EST (2 a.m. PST) on 2026-11-07, at an altitude of about 1,000 miles (1,700 kilometers) above Earth's surface.[2][5][6]
- **Gold (second spacecraft)**: Completed its close approach and approximately 9-minute trans-Mars injection burn at 6 a.m. EST on 2026-11-09, at an altitude of about 650 miles (1,000 kilometers) above Earth's surface.[2][5]

On 2026-11-09 around 9 a.m. EST, about three hours after closest approach and from a distance of about 38,500 miles from Earth, the second spacecraft (Gold) captured visible-light and infrared images of Earth's Western Hemisphere with its VIS camera, showing one of its solar panels in the foreground.[1][9] NASA published these parting images on 2026-11-23, alongside observations of the passing spacecraft captured by a Vantor Worldview Legion satellite on 2026-11-07 (at 3,380 to 3,480 miles) and 2026-11-09 (at 2,910 to 3,640 miles) and a photograph of the spacecraft passing overhead taken by a mission team member from near Los Angeles.[1] Science news outlets Nature World News and Starlust reported the image release on 2026-11-24.[8][9]

## ESCAPADE planned Mars arrival and science objectives

ESCAPADE is on a 10-month interplanetary cruise to Mars as of 2026-11-24, during which the spacecraft will perform trajectory correction maneuvers and can study solar wind dynamics between Earth and Mars.[2] Mars orbit insertion has not yet occurred; arrival is targeted for September 2027, with Mars orbit insertion burns of about 11 minutes planned for 2027-09-07 (Gold) and 2027-09-09 (Blue).[2][5]

After entering a capture orbit, mission teams plan to spend several months reducing and synchronizing the spacecraft orbits before starting the primary science mission in spring 2028.[4] By taking simultaneous measurements from two locations around Mars, ESCAPADE is designed to study how the solar wind interacts with Mars's magnetosphere, how energy and momentum move through the magnetic environment, and what processes drive atmospheric loss, informing preparations for future robotic and human missions to Mars.[1][2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the names of the two ESCAPADE spacecraft?

The two identical ESCAPADE orbiters, built by Rocket Lab for NASA and UC Berkeley, are named Blue and Gold. [3][5]

### When did ESCAPADE perform its Earth flybys and when were the Earth images released?

Blue performed its powered Earth flyby on 2026-11-07 and Gold completed its flyby on 2026-11-09. Gold captured the parting visible-light and infrared images of Earth on 2026-11-09 around 9 a.m. EST from about 38,500 miles away, and NASA publicly released those images on 2026-11-23. [1][2][5]

### Why did ESCAPADE wait near Earth for a year before heading to Mars?

When ESCAPADE launched on 2025-11-13, Earth and Mars were on opposite sides of the Sun. Both spacecraft entered a loiter orbit around Earth-Sun Lagrange point 2 (L2) and returned to Earth in November 2026 for powered gravity-assist flybys once the planets aligned. [2][4]

### Has ESCAPADE arrived at Mars yet?

No. As of 2026-11-24, both spacecraft are on a 10-month cruise toward Mars, with Mars orbit insertion targeted for September 2027 and the primary science mission planned to begin in spring 2028. [2][4]
