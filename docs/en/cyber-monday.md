# Cyber Monday

> Cyber Monday is the annual U.S. online shopping day held on the Monday after Thanksgiving, coined by the National Retail Federation in 2005. Adobe Analytics measured $14.25 billion in U.S. online spending on Cyber Monday 2025 and projected $15.1 billion for November 30, 2026, though faster growth has shifted toward earlier Black Friday deals.

Cyber Monday is an annual online shopping promotion held in the United States on the Monday immediately following Thanksgiving, closing the five-day Cyber Week period. The term was coined in 2005 by the National Retail Federation (NRF) after retailers observed recurring spikes in online shopping traffic and revenue on the post-Thanksgiving Monday as workers returned to office internet connections. According to Adobe Analytics, U.S. consumers spent a record $14.25 billion online on Cyber Monday 2025, making it the largest single e-commerce day of the year. For Cyber Monday on November 30, 2026, Adobe projected U.S. online spending to reach $15.1 billion, up 6.2% year on year, within a forecast $275.1 billion holiday season.

## Cyber Monday origin and evolution into Cyber Week

Cyber Monday originated in 2005 when the National Retail Federation (NRF) coined the term after noticing recurring surges in online retail traffic and revenue on the Monday following Thanksgiving.[4][5] At the time, online retailers sought a digital counterpart to brick-and-mortar Black Friday promotions, and many consumers shopped online upon returning to office desks where broadband connections were faster than home internet.[4][5]

Over two decades, Cyber Monday expanded into the anchor finale of Cyber Week, which Adobe defines as the five-day span from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday (November 26 to November 30 in 2026) and Salesforce tracks across seven days from the Tuesday before Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday (November 24 to November 30 in 2026).[1][3][6]

## Cyber Monday 2025 measured U.S. e-commerce results

In its January 7, 2026 holiday season report, Adobe Analytics reported that U.S. consumers spent a record $257.8 billion online across the 2025 holiday season, including $44.2 billion during Cyber Week (+7.7% year on year).[1] Cyber Monday remained the largest single e-commerce day of 2025 at $14.25 billion, up 7.1% year on year.[1]

Adobe noted that Cyber Monday's growth rate (+7.1%) was outpaced by Black Friday, which reached $11.8 billion (+9.1% year on year) as shoppers embraced earlier holiday discounts, while Thanksgiving Day generated $6.4 billion (+5.3% year on year).[1] Peak 2025 category discounts reached 30.9% off listed prices in electronics, 29.6% in toys, and 25.1% in apparel.[1]

## Cyber Monday 2026 sales forecasts and AI shopping trends

On September 28, 2026, Adobe forecast U.S. online holiday spending from November 1 to December 31, 2026, to reach a record $275.1 billion (+6.7% year on year), based on over 1 trillion visits to U.S. retail sites.[2][6]

Adobe highlighted that while Cyber Monday on November 30, 2026, is projected to remain the biggest single online day at $15.1 billion, Black Friday is forecast to grow faster (+9.2% versus +6.2%) because retailers roll out deep discounts earlier in November.[2][6] Separately, Salesforce predicted on November 19, 2026, that global Cyber Week online sales would reach $357 billion, with $75.8 billion influenced by AI and shopping agents (+13% from 2025) and AI-driven traffic to retailer sites up 104% year over year.[3]
