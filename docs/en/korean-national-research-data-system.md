# South Korea's National Research Data System

> South Korea's National Research Data System is a statutory lifecycle management framework established under the National Research Data Act taking effect on June 10, 2027. Approved on November 25-26, 2026, the policy requires newly launched national R&D projects from after June 2027 to submit Data Management Plans at application and register verification and designated large-facility datasets before final evaluation.

South Korea's National Research Data System (국가연구데이터 관리·활용 제도) is a statutory framework under the Act on the Promotion of Management and Utilization of National Research Data (Act No. 22076, taking effect June 10, 2027) designed to manage, register, and share research data produced through publicly funded R&D. Approved at the 15th Ministerial Meeting on Science and Technology (November 25-26, 2026) and announced by the Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) on November 27, 2026, the Basic Policy Direction requires researchers on newly launched national R&D projects starting after June 2027 to submit a Data Management Plan (DMP) at the application and agreement stage and register verification and designated large-facility datasets before final evaluation.

## Legal basis and November 2026 policy direction

The system is grounded in the Act on the Promotion of Management and Utilization of National Research Data (Act No. 22076), scheduled to take effect on June 10, 2027.[1] On November 27, 2026, the Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) announced that the 15th Ministerial Meeting on Science and Technology (held in written session on November 25-26) approved the Basic Policy Direction for National Research Data to establish a full-lifecycle management ecosystem and minimize confusion in research institutions ahead of enforcement.[2][4][5]

MSIT stated that publicly funded research data had previously often remained scattered on individual computers or external drives after papers and reports were completed, whereas structured high-quality datasets are needed for scientific AI training, verification, reproducibility, and interdisciplinary research.[5]

## What data must be registered, by whom, when, and what a DMP covers

Under the Basic Policy Direction, obligations apply to researchers conducting newly launched national R&D projects starting after June 2027.[3][4] At the project application and agreement stage, researchers must prepare and submit a Data Management Plan (DMP) outlining how research data will be managed and disclosed; research management agencies specify DMP requirements in project announcements and check compliance at final evaluation.[4][6]

| Requirement | Policy Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Covered projects | Newly launched national R&D projects after June 2027 |
| DMP submission timing | Project application and agreement stage |
| Dataset registration deadline | Before final project evaluation |
| Dataset structure | Primary data + metadata + accompanying interpretation data |
| Standard mandatory scope | Data verifying final-report results + nationally designated large-equipment data |
| Challenging projects exception | Intermediate process data also required for designated meaningful challenging and follow-up projects |

To avoid excessive administrative burden, mandatory registration is limited to datasets needed to verify and reproduce results reported in the final report, together with data produced by nationally designated large research equipment.[4][6] For designated innovative meaningful challenging projects and their follow-up projects, intermediate process data must also be registered so failure experiences can be preserved as shared assets.[4][6]

## Multi-layered repositories, default openness, and incentives

Researchers may choose where to register datasets among institutional repositories, domain-specific specialized centers, or the national integrated platform, depending on project characteristics and announcement terms.[4][6] These repositories will be linked into a multi-layered infrastructure enabling unified search and use on a single platform, supported by a designated National Research Data Center and domain-specific centers to be selected via open call in the first half of 2027.[4][6]

Registered research data is open by default, while non-disclosure is permitted for justifiable reasons such as national security or trade secrets.[6] The policy recommends international standard licenses allowing free reuse with attribution and provides evaluation incentives, including bonus points in future evaluations, for researchers and institutions that diligently register and share data.[3][4]

## Unverified implementation details as of November 2026

As of November 27, 2026, specific Enforcement Decree exemption criteria and finalized DMP and dataset submission forms have not yet been published and remain pending ahead of the June 10, 2027 statutory effective date.[1][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is South Korea's National Research Data System?

It is a national R&D data lifecycle management and sharing framework based on the National Research Data Act (effective June 10, 2027) and the Basic Policy Direction approved by the Ministerial Meeting on Science and Technology on November 25-26, 2026. [1][4]

### Who must register what data and when, and what goes into a Data Management Plan (DMP)?

Researchers on newly launched national R&D projects starting after June 2027 must submit a DMP detailing data management and disclosure plans at application and agreement. Before final evaluation, they must register a dataset (primary data, metadata, and accompanying data) covering final-report verification data and nationally designated large-equipment data, plus intermediate data for designated challenging projects. [3][4][6]

### Where do researchers register their datasets?

Researchers can choose among their institution's own repository, a domain-specific specialized center (to be selected in H1 2027), or the national integrated platform, all connected for unified search. [4][6]

### Must all registered research data be made public?

Open disclosure is the default principle with attribution-based international standard licenses recommended, but non-disclosure is allowed for justifiable reasons such as national security or trade secrets. [4][6]
