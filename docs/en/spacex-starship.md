# SpaceX Starship

> On November 13-14, 2026, SpaceX completed Integrated Flight Test 15 (IFT-15) of its upgraded Starship V3 rocket (Booster 22 and Ship 42, inscribed with 'Don't Panic') from Pad 2 at Starbase, Boca Chica, Texas. Powered by 33 Raptor 3 engines producing over 18 million pounds of liftoff thrust, IFT-15 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites and completed 14 orbits over a 22-hour mission before achieving the first-ever launch-tower 'chopsticks' catch of an orbital Starship upper stage on November 14, 2026 at 21:20:02 UTC (4:20 p.m. EST), while Booster 22 diverted to an offshore Gulf splashdown following a low-fuel/GNC abort.

SpaceX Starship (formally the Starship/Super Heavy launch system) is a two-stage-to-orbit, super heavy-lift launch vehicle and spacecraft designed and manufactured by Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX). Powered by liquid methane and liquid oxygen (methalox) Raptor engines and constructed from stainless steel, the vehicle consists of the Super Heavy first-stage booster (33 Raptor engines) and the Starship second-stage orbital spacecraft (also referred to as 'Ship'), both engineered for rapid recovery by the launch tower's mechanical 'Mechazilla' catch arms.

**Also known as:** Starship, Starship / Super Heavy, Starship V3, Starship HLS, IFT-15

## Overview and Starship V3 Architecture

The **Starship/Super Heavy** system is SpaceX's flagship super heavy-lift orbital launch vehicle, engineered to replace the partially reusable Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy families with a 100% reusable architecture capable of launching next-generation Starlink V3 broadband satellites, orbital data centers, and crewed missions to the Moon and Mars [1][4]. Both stages are fabricated from cryogenic stainless steel alloys and burn subcooled liquid methane and liquid oxygen (methalox) in full-flow staged-combustion **Raptor** engines [3][4].

By 2026, SpaceX transitioned flight operations to the upgraded **Starship V3** configuration [3]: - **Super Heavy (First Stage Booster)**: Powered by **33 Raptor 3 engines** generating more than **18 million pounds of thrust** at liftoff [3]. After hot-staging separation at approximately T+2 minutes 21 seconds (~40 miles / 65 km altitude), the booster performs a boostback burn and aerodynamic grid-fin descent to be caught in mid-air by the launch tower's mechanical arms (nicknamed **'Mechazilla'** or **'chopsticks'**) [3][4]. - **Starship / Ship (Second Stage)**: Functions as both the upper stage and an autonomous orbital spacecraft covered on its windward belly by thousands of black ceramic thermal-protection heat-shield tiles [4]. Rather than landing on legs or a runway like the NASA Space Shuttle orbiter, the operational Ship re-enters Earth's atmosphere belly-first, executes a landing flip-and-burn maneuver over the launch pad, and is caught directly by the Mechazilla chopsticks arms [3][4].

## Integrated Flight Test 15 (IFT-15) and First Orbital Ship Catch

Following twelve suborbital test flights and **IFT-14** (September 2026, which marked Starship's first full orbital insertion and completed nearly two orbits before a planned North Pacific splashdown after three hours), SpaceX launched **Integrated Flight Test 15 (IFT-15)** on **November 13, 2026** [3][4].

Flying **Booster 22** and **Ship 42** (inscribed with the phrase *'Don't Panic'* from Douglas Adams's *The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy*), the Starship V3 stack lifted off from **Pad 2** at Starbase, Boca Chica, Texas at **5:47 p.m. EST** (22:47 UTC / 4:47 p.m. CST) [3][4]. Following nominal hot-staging separation at T+2:21, Booster 22 initiated its return toward Starbase to attempt the program's first double-catch mission (having previously caught three Super Heavy boosters on IFT-5, IFT-7, and IFT-8) [3][4]. However, due to a low-fuel alarm and an automated guidance, navigation, and control (GNC) position check abort, Booster 22 autonomously diverted to an offshore splashdown and explosion in the Gulf of Mexico [3][4].

In orbit, **Ship 42** successfully deployed **26 Starlink V3 satellites** - which are physically too large to launch aboard Falcon 9 rockets - and remained in low Earth orbit for **22 hours**, completing **14 full orbits** around Earth [3][4]. On **November 14, 2026**, Ship 42 survived atmospheric re-entry over North America, ignited its Raptor engines for a vertical landing burn, and was caught cleanly by the Starbase Mechazilla tower chopsticks at **4:20 p.m. EST** (**21:20:02 UTC** / 3:20 p.m. CST), marking the first time in spaceflight history that an orbital upper stage was caught directly by a launch tower [3][4].

## NASA Artemis Program and the Human Landing System (HLS)

SpaceX holds multi-billion-dollar contracts with the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (**NASA**) first awarded in April 2021 (and expanded in 2022) to develop a lunar-lander derivative known as the **Starship Human Landing System (HLS)** under the **Artemis** program [5][6]. Because reaching lunar orbit with high payload mass requires refilling Starship's methalox tanks in low Earth orbit, SpaceX must still demonstrate in-space cryogenic propellant transfer between tanker and depot Starships [4].

Following the successful crewed lunar flyby of **Artemis II** in April 2026 (which carried astronauts Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch, and Jeremy Hansen 252,756 miles from Earth), NASA's updated Artemis architecture schedules Starship for two near-term crewed milestones [4][5]: - **Artemis III (Scheduled for 2027)**: A low-Earth-orbit demonstration mission carrying NASA astronauts Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio, Andre Douglas, and ESA astronaut Luca Parmitano aboard an Orion spacecraft. Orion will perform orbital rendezvous and docking tests with a standard Starship fitted with a docking adapter (without crew ingress into Starship) as well as with Blue Origin's Blue Moon MK2 lander (with crew ingress) [4][5]. - **Artemis IV and Artemis V (Scheduled for 2028)**: NASA's first crewed lunar surface landing since Apollo 17 is scheduled for **Artemis IV in 2028**, where astronauts will launch on SLS/Orion and transfer into a commercial Human Landing System to descend to the lunar south pole, followed by a second lunar landing on Artemis V later in 2028 [5].

## Launch Infrastructure and FAA Environmental Licensing

Under the Commercial Space Launch Act (51 U.S.C. §§ 50901–50923), all Starship/Super Heavy flights require vehicle operator licenses and National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) environmental reviews administered by the **Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Office of Commercial Space Transportation** [1][2].

To support its long-term goal of high-cadence reusable operations, SpaceX is developing a multi-site launch network [1][4][7]: 1. **Starbase (Boca Chica, Cameron County, Texas)**: SpaceX's primary manufacturing and flight-test complex, operating two orbital launch pads (including Pad 2, used for IFT-15) under FAA vehicle operator licensing and ongoing environmental assessments [1][3]. 2. **Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39A (LC-39A, Florida)**: Under a joint FAA and NASA Environmental Impact Statement (EIS), SpaceX has constructed a Starship launch and Mechazilla catch tower adjacent to the historic Apollo/Shuttle pad at LC-39A for orbital launches and booster/ship tower or droneship recoveries [4][7]. 3. **Vermilion Parish, Louisiana**: A newly announced third Starship launch complex planned on the Gulf Coast to expand operational launch cadence [4].

## Frequently asked questions

### What happened on SpaceX Starship's Integrated Flight Test 15 (IFT-15) in November 2026?

Launched on November 13, 2026 from Pad 2 at Starbase, Texas, IFT-15 sent Starship V3 (Ship 42) into low Earth orbit for 22 hours (14 orbits), deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and concluded on November 14, 2026 with the first-ever Mechazilla launch-tower 'chopsticks' catch of an orbital Starship upper stage. The Super Heavy first-stage booster (Booster 22) aborted its tower catch due to a low-fuel/GNC check and splashed down offshore in the Gulf. [3][4]

### How many engines and how much thrust does the Starship V3 Super Heavy booster have?

The Starship V3 Super Heavy first-stage booster is powered by 33 Raptor 3 engines burning subcooled liquid methane and liquid oxygen, generating more than 18 million pounds of thrust at liftoff. [3]

### How many times had SpaceX caught a Super Heavy booster or Starship before IFT-15?

Prior to IFT-15, SpaceX had successfully caught three Super Heavy first-stage boosters at the launch tower minutes after liftoff (on IFT-5, IFT-7, and IFT-8). IFT-15 on November 14, 2026 was the first attempt and first successful tower catch of the second-stage orbital Starship (Ship 42). [4]

### What is Starship's role in NASA's Artemis III and Artemis IV missions?

Under NASA's updated Artemis timeline (following the April 2026 Artemis II lunar flyby), Artemis III in 2027 will test low-Earth-orbit rendezvous and docking between the crewed Orion capsule and a standard Starship equipped with a docking adapter (as well as Blue Origin's Blue Moon MK2). The first crewed lunar surface landing using a commercial Human Landing System is scheduled for Artemis IV in 2028. [4][5][6]

### From which launch sites does SpaceX launch or plan to launch Starship?

SpaceX currently launches Starship from its Starbase facility at Boca Chica, Texas (including Pad 2), is completing FAA Environmental Impact Statement licensing for launches and tower catches at Kennedy Space Center Launch Complex 39A (LC-39A) in Florida, and has announced plans for a third launch site in Vermilion Parish, Louisiana. [1][4][7]
