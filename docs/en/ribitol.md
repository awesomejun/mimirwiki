# Ribitol (Welgemda)

> Ribitol (brand name Welgemda) is an oral solution developed by BridgeBio Pharma and approved by the U.S. FDA on November 23, 2026, as the first treatment for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 (LGMD2I/R9). Approved for adult and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kg, Welgemda demonstrated 12-month benefits over placebo on 100-meter timed-test velocity and forced vital capacity in the Phase 3 FORTIFY trial.

Ribitol (brand name Welgemda, development code BBP-418) is an oral small-molecule medicine developed by BridgeBio Pharma, Inc. and granted traditional approval by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on November 23, 2026, for the treatment of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 (LGMD2I/R9) in adult and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kg. Welgemda is the first FDA-approved therapy for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy. Approval was supported by 12-month interim analysis data from the Phase 3 FORTIFY trial (NCT05775848), which showed statistically significant improvements over placebo in 100-meter timed-test velocity (+0.27 m/s) and forced vital capacity percent predicted (+4.89%).

## Approved patient population and disease biology

On November 23, 2026, the FDA granted traditional approval to Welgemda (ribitol) for adult and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kg with limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 (LGMD2I/R9), making it the first FDA-approved treatment for any form of limb-girdle muscular dystrophy.[1][2][6] LGMD2I/R9 is a rare inherited muscle disease caused by mutations in the FKRP gene that impair the attachment of sugar molecules to alpha-dystroglycan, leaving muscle cells fragile and causing progressive weakness, difficulty walking, and impaired breathing.[1]

## Dosage, administration, and safety profile

According to the U.S. Prescribing Information (USPI), Welgemda is supplied as 9 g or 12 g packets for reconstitution as an oral solution taken twice daily with or without food at approximately the same times each day: 9 g twice daily for patients weighing 30 kg to 50 kg, and 12 g twice daily for patients weighing more than 50 kg.[4] The label lists no contraindications, and the most common adverse reactions (>=10%) observed in clinical trials were diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, arthralgia, dyspepsia, influenza, and flatulence.[4]

## What the Phase 3 FORTIFY clinical trial measured

FDA traditional approval was based on 12-month interim analysis results from the randomized, placebo-controlled Phase 3 FORTIFY trial (NCT05775848).[2][3][5]

| Phase 3 FORTIFY Endpoint (12-Month Interim Analysis) | Observed Treatment Benefit vs. Placebo |
| --- | --- |
| 100-meter timed test (100MTT) velocity | +0.27 m/s velocity benefit |
| Forced vital capacity (FVC) percent predicted | +4.89% benefit (lung function) |
| North Star Assessment for LGMD (NSAD) | Nominally significant motor improvement vs. decline on placebo |

Alongside approval, the FDA issued BridgeBio a Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher, and BridgeBio announced an immediate U.S. commercial launch supported by its ForgingBridges patient support program.[2]

## Unverified long-term outcomes as of November 2026

As of November 27, 2026, long-term clinical follow-up data beyond the 12-month FORTIFY interim analysis and real-world commercial access or payer coverage outcomes have not yet been established.[2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is ribitol (Welgemda)?

Ribitol (brand name Welgemda, BBP-418) is an oral solution developed by BridgeBio Pharma and approved by the U.S. FDA on November 23, 2026, as the first therapy for limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 2I/R9 (LGMD2I/R9). [1][2]

### Which patients are eligible for Welgemda and what did the clinical trial measure?

Welgemda is approved for adult and pediatric patients with LGMD2I/R9 weighing at least 30 kg. At the 12-month interim analysis of the Phase 3 FORTIFY trial, it measured 100-meter timed-test (100MTT) velocity (+0.27 m/s vs. placebo), forced vital capacity (FVC) percent predicted (+4.89% vs. placebo), and motor performance on the NSAD scale. [2][4]

### How is Welgemda dosed and what are the most common side effects?

Welgemda is reconstituted as an oral solution taken twice daily: 9 g twice daily for patients weighing 30-50 kg and 12 g twice daily for patients over 50 kg. The most common adverse reactions (>=10%) in clinical trials were diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea, arthralgia, dyspepsia, influenza, and flatulence. [4]

### Are long-term clinical follow-up results already available?

No. FDA approval was based on the 12-month interim analysis of FORTIFY, and longer-term follow-up outcomes and real-world access metrics remain unverified as of November 27, 2026. [2]
