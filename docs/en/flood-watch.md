# Flood Watch

> A Flood Watch ('Be Prepared') means conditions are favorable for flooding and flooding is possible, not yet certain or imminent. By contrast, a Flood Warning ('Take Action!') means flooding is imminent or already occurring.

A Flood Watch is an official public weather alert issued by the U.S. National Weather Service (NWS) when current or developing hydrometeorological conditions are favorable for flooding. Issuing a Flood Watch does not mean flooding is certain or already occurring; rather, it signals residents to prepare and monitor forecasts in case a Flood Warning is issued.

**Also known as:** NWS Flood Watch

## Key facts

- **Issuing agency:** U.S. National Weather Service (NWS), NOAA [1]
- **Core meaning:** Be Prepared: conditions are favorable for flooding; occurrence is neither certain nor imminent [1]
- **Typical lead time:** Several hours to days ahead (often within 36 hours) [3]
- **Higher alert level:** Flood Warning or Flash Flood Warning (imminent or occurring) [2]

## Basic facts

- **Alert name:** Flood Watch [1]
- **Issuing authority:** National Weather Service (NOAA) [2]
- **Call to action:** Be Prepared [2]
- **Coverage scope:** States, counties, rivers, portions of counties, or forecast points [3]
- **Warning confidence threshold:** Warnings and advisories are issued when confidence exceeds 80% [1]

## Flood Watch official definition

A Flood Watch is issued by the National Weather Service to indicate current or developing conditions that are favorable for flooding, where occurrence is neither certain nor imminent.[1][3] NWS summarizes a Flood Watch with the call to action 'Be Prepared' and notes that flooding is possible rather than guaranteed.[2]

Flood Watches are typically issued several hours to days ahead of possible flooding, and NWS hydrology guidance states they provide advance notice of possible flooding within 36 hours across states, counties, rivers, or specific forecast points.[1][3] When a river or stream is expected to be the primary source of flooding, forecast confidence can allow a Flood Watch to be issued several days in advance.[1]

## Flood Watch vs. Flood Warning and Advisory

A Flood Watch differs directly from a Flood Warning, Flash Flood Warning, and Flood Advisory in certainty and required public action.[1][2] Under NWS criteria, warnings and advisories are issued when confidence exceeds 80 percent that an event will occur, whereas a watch signals favorable conditions before onset is certain.[1]

A Flood Warning ('Take Action!') means flooding is imminent or already occurring, and NWS divides them into areal flood warnings and river flood warnings tied to forecast points.[2][3] A Flash Flood Warning ('Take Action!') applies to sudden, violent flooding developing within minutes to hours, including dam or levee failures, while a Flood Advisory ('Be Aware') covers nuisance flooding that is not severe enough for a warning but can still threaten life or property if caution is not exercised.[2][3]

## Flood Watch vs. Coastal Flood products

A Flood Watch for inland or riverine flooding is distinct from NWS coastal hazard products issued for shorelines and tidal waterways.[1][6] For example, on November 25, 2026, the NWS San Francisco Bay Area office issued a Coastal Hazard Message (CFW) keeping a Coastal Flood Warning in effect from 6 AM to 4 PM PST for Pacific Coast areas including the San Francisco Peninsula Coast and Coastal North Bay, alongside a High Surf Warning.[6]

That November 25, 2026 coastal bulletin warned of up to 2.7 feet of inundation above ground level in low-lying shoreline areas and 20-to-30-foot breaking waves, with a separate Coastal Flood Advisory expiring at 6 AM PST.[6] Because CFW bulletins address coastal inundation and surf rather than general inland rainfall flooding, a coastal flood product is not equivalent to a general Flood Watch.[1][6]

## Flood Watch preparedness and flood safety actions

Flood Watch alerts prompt residents to monitor changing conditions and determine what steps they will take if a warning is issued.[1] NWS notes that flooding is a coast-to-coast threat to parts of the United States and its territories nearly every day of the year.[4]

If flooding begins or a warning is issued, NWS instructs the public to stay informed through radio, television, NOAA Weather Radio, or official online updates, move immediately to higher ground from flood-prone or low-lying areas, obey evacuation orders, and avoid floodwaters.[5]

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a Flood Watch mean flooding is already happening?

No. Under National Weather Service definitions, a Flood Watch means conditions are favorable for flooding and it is possible, but flooding is neither certain nor imminent. [1][2]

### How is a Flood Watch different from a Flood Warning?

A Flood Watch means 'Be Prepared' because flooding is possible, while a Flood Warning means 'Take Action!' because flooding is imminent or already occurring. [2]

### Is a Coastal Flood Warning the same as a general Flood Watch?

No. Coastal Flood Warnings are Coastal Hazard products covering inundation near shorelines and tidal waterways, which are separate from general inland or riverine Flood Watches. [1][6]

### What should residents do when a Flood Watch is issued?

Residents should stay prepared by monitoring official NWS forecasts, NOAA Weather Radio, and local updates, and deciding what actions to take if a Flood Warning is issued. [1][2][5]
