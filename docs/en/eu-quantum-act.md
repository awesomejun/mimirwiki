# EU Quantum Act

> The EU Quantum Act is a European Commission legislative proposal introduced on November 25, 2026, to turn European quantum research into industrial products by 2030. It establishes the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative, coordinates shared research infrastructures and supply-chain monitoring, advances post-quantum cryptography adoption, and lays the groundwork for the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund.

The EU Quantum Act (COM(2026) 965) is a legislative regulation proposed by the European Commission on November 25, 2026, to strengthen the European Union's quantum technology industrial capacity, shared research infrastructure, supply-chain security, and transition to post-quantum cryptography. Building on the Quantum Europe Strategy adopted in July 2025, the proposal creates the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative to align EU and national programs across quantum computing, sensing, imaging, metrology, and communications. Alongside the regulation, the Commission laid the groundwork for the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF) to expand equity financing for strategic European technologies. As of November 26, 2026, the proposal is awaiting review by the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union.

## EU Quantum Act background and objectives

The EU Quantum Act was proposed by the European Commission on November 25, 2026, to build on the Quantum Europe Strategy adopted in July 2025 and support the EU's objective of strengthening industrial leadership in quantum technologies by 2030.[1][3] According to the European Commission, the EU hosts around one third of the world's quantum companies, yet faces three structural challenges: difficulty translating laboratory research into market-ready products and services, limited ability for start-ups and scale-ups to grow to industrial scale, and dependencies on concentrated or non-EU sources for critical components, technologies, and services.[3]

The proposal covers quantum computing, sensing, imaging, metrology, and communications, and recognizes photonic components as an enabling technology.[6] It sets two general objectives: sustaining the EU's scientific and technological base while strengthening industrial deployment capacity, and improving resilience, trusted quantum-safe deployment, and economic security across strategic quantum value chains and infrastructures.[3]

## EU Quantum Act pillars, research networks, and shared infrastructure

The EU Quantum Act is organized around three main priorities: research excellence, bringing quantum technologies into industrial and market use, and strengthening security, supply chains, and access to finance.[1][2] To align EU and national programs around shared priorities, the regulation establishes the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative.[2][3] Implementation mechanisms cited by the European Commission include a new network of European research institutes, shared quantum infrastructures, the Quantum Strategy Board, a new European Quantum Alliance bringing together public and private stakeholders, and the European network of quantum computers.[1]

According to Quantum Computing Report's summary of COM(2026) 965, the framework includes stage-gated Quantum Grand Challenges implemented through the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking and the Chips Joint Undertaking, a European Quantum Service Platform, Strategic Projects eligible for a Competitiveness Seal, Quantum Innovation Hubs, and national Competence and Acceleration Centres.[4] Reported technical targets include approximately 100 error-corrected logical qubits per system by 2030, GNSS-denied positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) by 2030, 50-kilometre metropolitan quantum networks, and EuroQCI terrestrial-space quantum key distribution networks by 2031.[4]

## EU Quantum Act supply-chain monitoring and post-quantum cryptography

The EU Quantum Act addresses both industrial opportunities and cybersecurity risks posed by future quantum computers.[3] The European Commission noted that sufficiently powerful quantum computers could undermine widely used cryptographic systems, affecting public administrations, critical infrastructures, sensitive communications, digital identities, and financial services.[3]

To address those risks, the EU Quantum Act proposes measures to monitor quantum supply chains, identify critical suppliers and components, and reduce strategic dependencies.[1] It also coordinates the transition to post-quantum cryptography (PQC), adopting new encryption methods designed to protect data and digital systems against future quantum computing capabilities.[1][2] Quantum Computing Report noted that the proposal links Union-controlled procurement rules, NIS2 post-quantum cryptography mandates, and critical-component monitoring.[4]

## EU Quantum Act financing groundwork and legislative status

Alongside the EU Quantum Act, the European Commission laid the groundwork for the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF), intended to provide equity financing for technologies considered essential to European technological sovereignty and economic security, including quantum systems, artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and cloud infrastructure.[1][4][6] Quantum Computing Report reported that STEF is envisioned as a public-private equity vehicle with the European Investment Bank (EIB) Group and Member States under the 2028 to 2034 Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF), targeting up to 50 billion euros across sovereign technologies, while the Commission's impact assessment identified 12.9 billion to 18.1 billion euros in European quantum financing needs for 2028 to 2034.[4]

Optics & Photonics News noted that neither the Quantum Act proposal nor the STEF groundwork guarantees a dedicated quantum budget, as funding amounts and allocations remain to be determined.[6] As of November 26, 2026, the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union are to consider the legislative proposal; final negotiation outcomes and an entry-into-force date have not yet been established.[5]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the EU Quantum Act?

The EU Quantum Act (COM(2026) 965) is a legislative regulation proposed by the European Commission on November 25, 2026, to scale European quantum research into commercial industrial capacity, strengthen supply chains, and coordinate the transition to post-quantum cryptography. [1][2][4]

### How does the EU Quantum Act support shared research infrastructure?

The proposal establishes the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative to align EU and national programs, supported by a network of European research institutes, shared quantum infrastructures, the European network of quantum computers, the Quantum Strategy Board, and a European Quantum Alliance. [1][2]

### How does the EU Quantum Act address post-quantum cryptography?

The proposal coordinates the transition to post-quantum cryptography to protect public administrations, critical infrastructures, sensitive communications, digital identities, and financial services from future quantum computers capable of breaking current encryption. [1][3]

### What is the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF) announced alongside the EU Quantum Act?

The Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF) is a planned equity financing vehicle laid out alongside the Quantum Act to support strategic European technologies including quantum systems, artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and cloud infrastructure. Exact budget allocations remain to be finalized. [1][4][6]

### Is the EU Quantum Act already in force?

No. As of November 26, 2026, the EU Quantum Act is a European Commission proposal submitted to the European Parliament and the Council of the European Union for consideration, and its final text and effective date have not been determined. [5]
