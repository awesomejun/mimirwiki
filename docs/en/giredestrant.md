# Giredestrant (Wilserdly)

> On November 16, 2026, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved Wilserdly (giredestrant, NDA 220791) in combination with everolimus for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer (as detected by the companion diagnostic FoundationOne Liquid CDx) whose disease recurred during or after adjuvant endocrine therapy or progressed on at least one metastatic endocrine regimen. In the Phase 3 evERA Breast Cancer trial (NCT05306340), giredestrant plus everolimus reduced the risk of disease progression or death by 62% in the ESR1-mutated population (HR 0.38; p < 0.0001; median PFS 10.0 vs 5.5 months). A separate Priority Review NDA for adjuvant stage I-III breast cancer (lidERA trial) has a PDUFA target date of November 30, 2026.

Giredestrant (marketed under the brand name Wilserdly) is an oral next-generation selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) and full estrogen receptor (ER) antagonist developed by Genentech, Inc., a member of the Roche Group. Taken as a 30 mg once-daily oral capsule in combination with the mTOR inhibitor everolimus, giredestrant binds to the ligand binding domain of estrogen receptor alpha, antagonizes the activity of the receptor encoded by wild-type and mutated estrogen receptor 1 (ESR1), and induces degradation of the receptor protein, according to its prescribing information.

## Indication, Project Orbis Approval, and Companion Diagnostic

**Giredestrant** (brand name **Wilserdly**, pronounced *wil-SERD-lee*) is an oral estrogen receptor antagonist and selective estrogen receptor degrader (SERD) approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on **November 16, 2026** (NDA 220791; CDER Novel Drug Therapy Approval #51 of 2026) [1][2][3][4].

Wilserdly is indicated **in combination with everolimus** for the treatment of adults with **estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, estrogen receptor 1 (*ESR1*)-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer**, as detected by an FDA-authorized companion diagnostic test, whose disease has recurred during or after completion of endocrine-based adjuvant therapy or has progressed on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the metastatic setting [3][4].

Concurrent with the drug approval, the FDA approved **FoundationOne Liquid CDx** (Foundation Medicine, Inc.) as the companion diagnostic liquid biopsy assay to detect *ESR1* ligand-binding domain mutations in plasma circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) [3][8]. The review of NDA 220791 (originally received December 18, 2025) was conducted under **Project Orbis** in collaboration with **Health Canada (HC)** and **Israel's Ministry of Health (IMoH)** using the Assessment Aid, and was approved four weeks ahead of the FDA goal date [3][5]. Under section 505(o) of the FD&C Act, the approval letter includes Postmarketing Requirement **PMR 5064-1** requiring Genentech to conduct a clinical pharmacokinetic trial of multi-dose giredestrant on a sensitive **CYP2C9** substrate, with trial completion scheduled for March 2030 and final report submission for July 2030 [5]. In addition, Genentech has a separate NDA under FDA Priority Review for giredestrant as an adjuvant endocrine therapy in intermediate-to-high-risk stage I–III ER+/HER2- breast cancer based on the Phase 3 **lidERA** study, with a target action date of **November 30, 2026** [7][9].

## Mechanism of Action and Oral Combination Regimen

Hormone receptor (HR)-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer accounts for approximately 70% of breast cancer cases, and up to **40%** of patients with HR-positive metastatic breast cancer develop mutations in the ***ESR1* gene** that drive resistance and disease progression [7][8]. Genentech describes activation of the **PI3K/AKT/mTOR (PAM)** signaling pathway, with continued dependency on ER, as a key mechanism of resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitor and endocrine therapy [7].

According to its prescribing information, giredestrant is an estrogen receptor antagonist that binds to the ligand binding domain of ERα, antagonizes the activity of ERα encoded by wild-type and mutated *ESR1*, and induces degradation of the ERα protein [4]. Genentech describes it as a next-generation, potent, oral SERD and full antagonist [7]. Genentech states that the combination with **everolimus** (an mTOR inhibitor) targets two different signaling pathways as an oral regimen without the need for injections [7].

**Dosage and Administration**: The recommended dosage of Wilserdly is **30 mg taken orally once daily** (one 30 mg capsule swallowed whole, with or without food, at approximately the same time each day) in combination with everolimus (10 mg orally once daily in the pivotal trial) until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity [3][4]. Pre- or peri-menopausal women and men should also receive a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist [3][4]. Note that Wilserdly capsules contain lactose monohydrate, and dosage reduction below 30 mg is not recommended (adverse reactions are managed via dose interruption or permanent discontinuation) [4].

## Pivotal Phase 3 evERA Breast Cancer Trial (NCT05306340)

Clinical efficacy was established in **evERA Breast Cancer** (`NCT05306340`), a Phase 3, randomized, open-label, multicenter trial published in *The New England Journal of Medicine* that enrolled **373 adults** with ER-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer previously treated with a CDK4/6 inhibitor and endocrine therapy, including **207 patients** with centrally confirmed *ESR1* mutations via FoundationOne Liquid CDx [3][6][7]. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive **giredestrant 30 mg + everolimus 10 mg** once daily ($N = 183$) versus **physician's choice of endocrine therapy (PCET: exemestane, fulvestrant, or tamoxifen) + everolimus 10 mg** once daily ($N = 190$) [3]:

| evERA Efficacy Population & Endpoint | Giredestrant 30 mg + Everolimus | PCET + Everolimus | Hazard Ratio / Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ***ESR1*-Mutated (ESR1m) Median PFS** ($N = 207$, co-primary) | **10.0 months** (95% CI: 8.1-12.9) | **5.5 months** (95% CI: 3.8-5.6) | **HR 0.38** (95% CI: 0.27-0.54; $p < 0.0001$; **62% risk reduction**) [3][7] |
| **ESR1m Objective Response Rate (ORR)** | **27%** (95% CI: 18%-37%) | **14%** (95% CI: 8%-22%) | 27% vs 14% [3] |
| **Intent-to-Treat (ITT) Median PFS** ($N = 373$, co-primary) | **8.8 months** | **5.5 months** | **HR 0.56** (95% CI: 0.44-0.71) [9] |
| **Overall Survival (OS, Interim Analysis)** | Immature at PFS cutoff | Immature at PFS cutoff | Numerically favored giredestrant (ESR1m HR 0.62; ITT HR 0.69) [3][9] |

The FDA reported a statistically significant improvement in PFS in patients whose tumors had *ESR1* mutations, and the approved indication is for *ESR1*-mutated disease [3][4].

## Warnings, Precautions, and Adverse Reactions

The FDA Prescribing Information for Wilserdly carries no contraindications (`None`) and highlights two primary Warnings and Precautions in Section 5 [4]: 1. **Bradycardia (Section 5.1)**: Wilserdly can cause a decrease in heart rate. Clinicians should monitor heart rate more frequently in patients with pre-existing bradycardia or concomitant medications known to cause bradycardia. For symptomatic bradycardia, Wilserdly should be withheld until resolution and contributing concomitant medications adjusted; it must be permanently discontinued for life-threatening bradycardia if no contributing concomitant drug is identified [4]. 2. **Embryo-Fetal Toxicity (Section 5.2)**: Based on animal reproduction studies showing embryo-fetal mortality and structural abnormalities at human-equivalent exposures, Wilserdly can cause fetal harm. Females of reproductive potential must use effective nonhormonal contraception during treatment and for **2 weeks after the last dose**, and patients are advised not to breastfeed [4][9].

In the evERA trial, the most common **adverse reactions ($\ge 20\%$)** in the giredestrant plus everolimus arm were **stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis, and nausea** [3]. The most common **laboratory abnormalities ($\ge 20\%$)** were decreased hemoglobin, decreased lymphocytes, decreased white blood cell count, increased AST, increased ALT, decreased neutrophils, decreased platelets, decreased calcium, decreased potassium, increased creatinine, and decreased sodium [3].

## Frequently asked questions

### What is giredestrant (Wilserdly) approved for?

On November 16, 2026, the U.S. FDA approved Wilserdly (giredestrant) in combination with everolimus for adults with estrogen receptor (ER)-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer whose disease recurred during or after adjuvant endocrine therapy or progressed on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the metastatic setting. [1][3][4]

### How effective was giredestrant plus everolimus in the Phase 3 evERA trial?

In the Phase 3 evERA Breast Cancer trial (NCT05306340), giredestrant 30 mg plus everolimus 10 mg reduced the risk of disease progression or death by 62% compared with physician's choice of endocrine therapy plus everolimus in patients with ESR1-mutated tumors (hazard ratio 0.38; p < 0.0001). Median progression-free survival was 10.0 months versus 5.5 months, and objective response rate was 27% versus 14%. [3][7][9]

### What companion diagnostic test is used to select patients for Wilserdly?

The FDA approved Foundation Medicine's FoundationOne Liquid CDx liquid biopsy assay as the companion diagnostic to detect ESR1 ligand-binding domain mutations in plasma circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) prior to initiating Wilserdly plus everolimus. [3][8]

### What is the recommended dosage of Wilserdly?

The recommended dose of Wilserdly is 30 mg (one capsule swallowed whole) taken orally once daily with or without food in combination with everolimus until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Dose reduction below 30 mg is not recommended. [3][4]

### What are the main side effects and warnings for giredestrant (Wilserdly)?

Wilserdly carries warnings and precautions for bradycardia (decreased heart rate) and embryo-fetal toxicity (requiring effective contraception during treatment and for 2 weeks after the final dose). The most common adverse reactions (>=20%) when combined with everolimus are stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhea, musculoskeletal pain, pneumonitis, and nausea. [3][4]
