# Pablo Hernández de Cos

> Pablo Hernández de Cos is a Spanish central banker, General Manager of the Bank for International Settlements since July 2025, and former Governor of the Bank of Spain (2018 to 2024). On November 26, 2026, eurozone member states agreed to recommend him as the next President of the European Central Bank to succeed Christine Lagarde.

Pablo Hernández de Cos is a Spanish economist and central banker who serves as General Manager of the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) and previously served as Governor of the Bank of Spain from 2018 to June 2024. On November 26, 2026, European Council President António Costa announced that euro area member states agreed to recommend Hernández de Cos as the next President of the European Central Bank (ECB) to succeed Christine Lagarde. Earlier in his career, he chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) and the Advisory Technical Committee of the European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB). Formal appointment by the European Council is expected to be finalized in December 2026 following required institutional consultations.

## Pablo Hernández de Cos education and Bank of Spain tenure

Pablo Hernández de Cos was born in Madrid on January 20, 1971.[3] According to his official Bank of Spain curriculum vitae, he earned a degree in Economics and Business Sciences from CUNEF (Universidad Complutense de Madrid) in 1993, a Law degree from Universidad Nacional de Educación a Distancia (UNED) in 1994, a Ph.D. in Economics from Universidad Complutense de Madrid in 2004, and completed an executive management program at IESE Business School in 2009.[3]

Hernández de Cos served as Governor of the Bank of Spain from 2018 until June 2024.[1][3] In that capacity, he sat on the Governing Council and General Council of the European Central Bank (ECB), served as Alternate Governor for Spain at the International Monetary Fund (IMF), and held membership in the Financial Stability Board (FSB) and the General Board of the European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB).[3]

## Pablo Hernández de Cos international committees and BIS leadership

Alongside his national central bank duties, Pablo Hernández de Cos chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) starting in 2019 and chaired the Advisory Technical Committee (ATC) of the European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB).[3] He also served as Chair of the Board of Governors of the Center for Latin American Monetary Studies (CEMLA) from 2023 and as a member of the Advisory Board of the Financial Stability Institute (FSI) at the Bank for International Settlements.[3]

On November 11, 2024, the Board of Directors of the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) announced the appointment of Hernández de Cos as its next General Manager for a five-year term effective July 1, 2025, succeeding Agustín Carstens.[1][2]

## Pablo Hernández de Cos November 2026 ECB presidency recommendation

On November 26, 2026, European Council President António Costa announced that euro area member states agreed to recommend Pablo Hernández de Cos as the next President of the European Central Bank (ECB).[5][6][7] He was recommended to succeed Christine Lagarde, who was appointed in October 2019 and announced she would step down early in February 2027, eight months before her term expires.[5] CNN Business and Al Jazeera reported that if formally confirmed, Hernández de Cos will become the first Spaniard to lead the ECB, which manages monetary policy for the 21 countries using the euro.[5][6]

As part of the same announcement, Costa stated that eurozone member states also recommended Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier for positions on the ECB Executive Board.[6]

## Pablo Hernández de Cos remaining ECB appointment steps

Under European Central Bank governance rules, all six members of the ECB Executive Board, including the President, Vice-President, and four other members, are formally appointed by the European Council acting by a qualified majority.[4] Following the November 26, 2026 euro area recommendation, the appointment of Hernández de Cos and the two Executive Board nominees requires formal approval, which European Council President António Costa stated is expected to be finalized at the December 2026 European Union summit.[5][6]

As of November 27, 2026, the formal European Council appointment has not yet been completed.[5][6]
