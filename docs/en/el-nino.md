# El Niño

> El Niño is the warm phase of the Pacific Ocean's El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) climate pattern, characterized by above-average equatorial sea surface temperatures that reshape global weather. On November 12, 2026, NOAA's Climate Prediction Center projected a greater than 79% chance of a strong-to-very-strong El Niño persisting through January to March 2027, favoring a warmer northern U.S. and wetter South.

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a coupled ocean-atmosphere climate pattern in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean that influences weather patterns worldwide. During El Niño, warmer-than-average sea surface temperatures in the equatorial Pacific shift atmospheric circulation, including moving the winter jet stream southward and eastward across the contiguous United States. In its November 12, 2026 ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, the NOAA Climate Prediction Center (CPC) maintained an El Niño Advisory and reported a greater than 79% chance that a strong-to-very-strong El Niño persists through January to March 2027. NOAA's 2026-2027 U.S. Winter Outlook favors warmer-than-average temperatures across the northern United States and wetter-than-average conditions across the southern United States and Eastern Seaboard.

**Also known as:** El Nino, ENSO warm phase

## El Niño ocean-atmosphere mechanism

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), a natural climate pattern in the Pacific Ocean that affects weather around the globe.[5] During an El Niño event, sea surface temperatures across the central and eastern equatorial Pacific rise above average, accompanied by a deeper-than-average thermocline and above-average subsurface ocean temperatures.[2]

Meteorologists monitor El Niño intensity using regional sea surface temperature anomaly indices across the equatorial Pacific, including Niño-3.4, Niño-3, and Niño-1+2, alongside subsurface temperature anomalies averaged between 180° and 100°W.[2] Experimental modeling from NOAA's Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Laboratory (GFDL) also tracks how Pacific ocean-atmosphere coupling evolves across seasonal lead times.[3]

## El Niño status in NOAA's November 2026 diagnosis

In its November 12, 2026 ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, the NOAA Climate Prediction Center (CPC) maintained an El Niño Advisory and reported that El Niño continued to strengthen, with sea surface temperature anomalies exceeding 4.0 degrees Celsius extending farther westward across the eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean.[1][2]

## El Niño influence on U.S. regional temperature and precipitation probabilities

El Niño's equatorial Pacific warming links to U.S. regional weather probabilities by shifting the winter jet stream southward and eastward across the contiguous United States.[4] According to NOAA's U.S. Winter Outlook for December 2026 through February 2027, released on October 15, 2026, a very strong El Niño tilts the odds toward warmer-than-average temperatures across the northern United States and wetter-than-average conditions across the southern United States, from California to Florida, as well as along the Eastern Seaboard.[4]

National Weather Service Director Ken Graham noted in NOAA's release that higher chances of rain and snow across the South and East can bring drought relief to areas including the East, Southern Great Plains, and Southwest, while also increasing storminess.[4] TIME reported on November 25, 2026, on early signs of strengthening El Niño effects along U.S. coastal and regional weather patterns.[6]

## El Niño seasonal outlook uncertainty

NOAA seasonal outlooks express shifted probabilities rather than deterministic weather guarantees for any single storm or city.[4] As of November 27, 2026, the realized winter 2026-2027 peak intensity of El Niño and actual regional precipitation and storm impacts across the United States remain unverified until the winter season concludes.[2][4]

## Frequently asked questions

### What is El Niño?

El Niño is the warm phase of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO) climate pattern in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean, characterized by above-average sea surface temperatures that alter global atmospheric circulation. [2][5]

### How do Pacific sea surface temperature changes connect to U.S. regional precipitation probabilities?

Warming in the equatorial Pacific shifts the winter jet stream southward and eastward across the contiguous United States, tilting seasonal odds toward wetter-than-average conditions across the southern U.S. (from California to Florida) and the Eastern Seaboard, and warmer-than-average temperatures in the northern U.S. [4]

### What did NOAA report in its November 12, 2026 El Niño update?

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center maintained an El Niño Advisory, reported Niño-3.4 at +2.5°C, Niño-3 at +3.6°C, and Niño-1+2 at +4.4°C, and projected a greater than 79% chance that a strong-to-very-strong El Niño persists through January to March 2027. [1][2]

### Does El Niño guarantee drought relief or specific storms in winter 2026-2027?

No. NOAA's outlook indicates elevated probabilities of wetter conditions in the South and East and potential drought improvement, but actual winter intensity and localized impacts remain unconfirmed until observed. [4]
